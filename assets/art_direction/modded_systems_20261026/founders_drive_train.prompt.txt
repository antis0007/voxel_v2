SHEET TITLE: FOUNDERS' DRIVE TRAIN.
Six original modular workshop assemblies in three columns by two rows, each shown three-quarter isometric and clearly separated. Take conceptual inspiration from the repository's Create, Immersive Engineering, GregTech and Mekanism references: visible kinetic routing, supported multiblocks, processing stages, service clearances and distinct power interfaces. Do not reproduce their specific machine skins. These are Axiom's makers maintaining the braid's material supply.
This branch is heavier and more mechanical than the optical fabrication sheet, yet uses the SAME palette and exact material language. Walnut/iron structures and controlled copper coils, ivory ceramic insulation, worn contact edges only. Strongly differentiate shaft couplings from electric cable lugs. Never put gears on a panel merely for decoration. Every visible gear should connect to a supported shaft or working part. Quiet housings, sparse meaningful fasteners, no unreadable piping spaghetti, no magical floating belts.
1 "Right-angle gearbox": squat open-top dark iron bearing housing with two large readable meshing gears on perpendicular supported shafts, a simple access lid propped beside it, one walnut mounting foot. Gear pitch and physical contact must look plausible.
2 "Transfer belt": short inclined moving belt assembly on two broad visible rollers, a little tensioning shoe, sturdy support feet and a single workpiece riding its upper surface. Show the return belt underneath.
3 "Ram press": tall simple iron portal frame, a central ram clearly guided between two uprights, a separate upper shaft/crank input and a lower die bed with removable stock tray. Continuous support down to its feet, not a collection of oversized wooden blocks.
4 "Heated mixer": broad copper-lined insulated vessel, vertical paddle drive visibly connected to an overhead shaft, separate modest lower heat source and a short exhaust neck, a physical drain at bottom. Heat is located at process vessel only.
5 "Tool deployer": compact articulated cantilever arm with two clear hinges, a replaceable small steel tool, a reachable work tray below and a modest protective stop. Tool, joint and workpiece have different silhouettes. Original industrial helper, not a humanoid.
6 "Cable transformer": heavy rectangular laminated dark core with two visibly different copper winding groups, pale ceramic bushings and distinct supply/return cable lugs, on a simple iron service base. No giant gem, no permanently glowing coil.
At the bottom, one small connected installation: an external shaft on supported bearings drives the gearbox and ram press, while a separately routed thin signal lead reaches a simple limit switch. Leave physical access room at the press. Beside it two tiny SAME-camera state images labelled "READY" and "STALLED": raised ram and calm bearing in READY; obstructed workpiece with stopped ram and a local warm bearing indication in STALLED. No invented explosion or full-object red shader. A few simple curved arrows may indicate motion; do not use animation blur.
