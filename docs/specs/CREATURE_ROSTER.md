# Creature roster brief

Source brief supplied by the owner on 2026-11-03. Preserved as reference input;
implementation status is tracked at the end, not inside the brief.

Direction: absurd creatures with coherent behaviour, dangerous consequences,
and weaknesses the player can discover through observation and experimentation.
Humane absurdity, cosmic indifference, dry technically informed sarcasm.

## 1. Old Copper, the Thunderhen

A six-metre copper-feathered hen nested on an abandoned weather station. She
has adopted its charged stormstones as eggs. The station replenishes her
charge; she protects it because it holds her eggs. The village wants her moved.
Several workshops have quietly connected their power lines to the fence.

> "Please move the hen. My brother wants you to kill it. His workshop isn't
> connected to the fence."

Silhouette: broad heavy body over substantial legs, small head that stays
steady while the rest moves, copper and rust plumage in stepped clusters, a
comb that brightens with charge, neck feathers that rise one by one before a
discharge, storm crystals tangled in the plumage. Everyday behaviour visible
before the fight: scratching, rearranging the nest, inspecting shiny objects.

| Attack | Seen | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| The Look | Freeze, head tilt, a crackling patch follows one player, then locks before lightning. | Move after the lock, divert through a grounding rod, place the strike over another threat. |
| Investigative pecking | Neck retracts, short charge, three heavy pecks along a committed line. | Sidestep, strike the lowered head, arrange something unpleasant at the end of the run. |
| Scratching | One foot lifts, two broad furrows torn behind her. | Move around her front, use the trenches later. |
| Wing shakedown | Wings rise, charged feathers loosen and are thrown. | Shelter, pull a feather with the gravity spell, throw it somewhere useful. |
| Dust bath | Crouch and roll; arcs spread around the disturbed ground. | Get outside the arcs, redirect, use the long recovery. |

Lightning is avoided during preparation; once the bolt happens it resolves at
once. Electrical interactions are explicit: rods, feathers, stormstones and
machinery show their state.

She returns to the nest to recharge; charge travels visibly from the stormstones
into her feathers. Openings: attack while stationary, disconnect the station,
move a stormstone, capture the discharge. Grounding her is one strategy among
several. A strong enclosure may contain her. Heavy machinery is an advantage.

Nonlethal victory: build a replacement nest away from the charging equipment,
move the stormstones, distract her, discharge her through a grounding perch.
Settled, she is an enormous territorial animal with occasional electrical
problems. No health threshold required.

Reward: storm quills, an electrical interaction for wands and machinery:
capture a limited discharge, hold it visibly, release it into a chosen
conductor. Both routes unlock the same technology.

## 2. Redbrick, the Furnace Dragon

Inhabits an old furnace complex that was built around him: ducts take his
breath, recovery chambers collect waste heat, conveyors deliver fuel. He
understands the machinery and regards everything combustible as his property.

> "You built a town around a furnace and expected the heat to be free. I admire
> the ambition."

Look: heavy forelimbs polished by furnace brick, ceramic growths between dark
scales, soot under the jaw, chains and ducts attached where he tolerates them,
a belly that glows after feeding, a tail disappearing through a wall.

Fight: when he inhales, connected ducts brighten in sequence, shutters tremble,
a floor grate glows before the discharge. Players open dampers, break ducts,
cover grates, force discharges through corroded machinery, cool exposed plates,
reach his flanks while he is committed to a duct. Damaged connections free him
to move and make industrial attacks less predictable but still signalled.

Resolutions: direct victory (furnace components, dragon material); engineering
route (restore intake and pressure controls); negotiation (a practical reason to
cooperate; he remembers useful actions, broken promises, and who shot him in
the face). Reward: dragon-scale nozzle (narrow cutting jet or broad fan) and
recovered heat-exchange machinery.

> Temperature above the green band may damage equipment. Temperature above the
> red band may transfer ownership.

## 3. The Backup Sun

An underground agricultural district whose lighting plant still tries to finish
the morning cycle. Sensors detect cold soil; the response is additional
daylight. Trees have living bark on one side and a glassy burn scar on the
other. A closed mechanical flower hangs above; its petals unfold.

Fight: rotating shutters and reflectors expose sectors. Before a sector becomes
dangerous: reflector turns, a strip of light crosses the floor, surfaces
brighten and crack, the shutter opens. The problem is maintaining a route
through changing shelter: dig trenches, topple pillars, move slabs with the
gravity spell, rotate reflectors onto machinery, breach irrigation walls, warm
a sensor so a sector receives less light. Cover has understandable endurance.

Escalation: the controller compensates for blocked reflectors; shaded sectors
receive more attention. Resolution: destroy the assembly, cut its supply, or
restore a safe cycle (preserving the growing site). Reward: light-focusing
components for beam spells, optical machinery, advanced agriculture.

> Do not inspect the daylight directly.

## 4. The Reservoir Beast

A freshwater animal whose shell was incorporated into the waterworks over
successive repairs: gates, walkways, encrusted pipes, a derelict pump house.
The settlement calls its movements "subsidence". Tortoise-like, powerful neck,
massive bracing limbs, weight and hesitation.

Attacks: forward discharge sweeping a marked channel, bracing lunge against
supports, shell tilt changing usable walkways, withdrawal pulling loose objects
toward an intake, sudden rise exposing vulnerable parts. Players lower pressure
through bypass channels, detach jammed gates, climb the shell, lure a brace into
weak masonry. Water is a bounded set of channels, levels and local impulses.

Reason: a juvenile trapped beneath the intake works, discovered through
behaviour (the adult returns to one gate; smaller impacts answer from below).
Freeing it gives the creature a reason to leave and opens the older district.
Reward: pressure accumulator; the larger reward is access.

> "Movement remains within historical tolerances." (amended several times)

## 5. The Long Guest

Something hunts the routes between Manyfold chambers. Its long body passes
through several rooms; the player sees a limb through one doorway, a flank
through another. The same injury appears through two openings. It has learned
that people follow help beacons.

Design restrained: narrow body, pale overlapping plates, limbs folded, a head
nearly flush with a doorframe, settling-architecture movements. Fight: a few
understandable chambers with a stable entrance anchor. Each doorway warns before
an attack (plates scrape, dust falls, a limb shows). Players anchor connections,
close retreat routes, place lures, strike exposed flanks through other doors,
redirect lunges into terrain. One coherent adversary; damage persists.

It occasionally repeats a practical phrase heard from previous travellers.
Reward: a Wayhook that temporarily anchors a portal mouth. A restrained Crimson
King connection: deeper in the Manyfold it refuses a particular passage.

> Open one door at a time.

## 6. Ordinary enemies

- Hingecrab: hermit crab in a salvaged door and frame. Door rattles before it
  opens and the crab lunges. Attack through the opening, wedge the hinge, pull
  the door sideways, offer a better shell.
- Archback: stone-shelled beetle bred to brace tunnels. Posture shows whether
  it supports something; withdrawal causes a readable local collapse. Build a
  replacement support, lure it, or fight from a safe position.
- Scrap kite: scavenging bird that drops what it carries. Load visible on the
  approach; heavy loads fly slowly. Catch, shelter, intercept, lure. Nests mark
  ruined industry.
- Surveyor: three-legged machine that plants two stakes, draws a cutting plane,
  executes it. Move a stake and the cut changes; break one and it resets; lure
  an enemy into the plane.
- Rubble witch: salvage professional with suspended masonry as ammunition. A
  slab shakes, rises, rotates, launches. Anchor it, steal it with gravity,
  destroy the scaffold, negotiate.
- Cairn hound: builds a stone body around a small core. Picks up a stone before
  spitting it; cannot bite while carrying. Packs keep cairns around dens.
- Kettleback: hot-spring animal with mineral plates and cookware. Dorsal lid
  whistles before a steam blast; side vents show direction. Warm habitat
  supports useful plants.
- Ash gardener: cultivates heat fungi around vents. Plants glowing spores in a
  line; they swell and burst. Water extinguishes spores; persistent flooding
  makes it abandon the patch.
- Company salvagers: a human faction using the player's tools. Brace, retrieve
  wounded, build cover, cut supports, retreat when unprofitable. May offer a
  deal first.
- The Second Caver: a thin clay creature whose body makes the mining sound.
  Three scraping beats and visible dust from the surface it will emerge
  through; strikes near the last strong impact. No joke attached.

## 7. Encounters together

- Ruined weather station: scrap kites stole conductive parts from Old Copper's
  station; a Surveyor keeps cutting fence sections.
- Excavation gone wrong: salvagers removing an Archback from under a machine a
  rubble witch claims.
- Cistern: kettlebacks in warm pools, ash gardeners at vents, the Reservoir
  Beast changing access to both.
- Deep mine: ordinary mining sounds, then one extra rhythm after the player
  stops.

## 8. Humour rules

Each character has a concrete concern. Expertise varies. Useful information
lives inside the joke. Urgent combat information stays plain. Silence and
sincerity are allowed. Short observations; one good line per bestiary entry.

## 9. Design rules

Preparation must work. Destruction must preserve a route to victory. Scale
comes from a few consequential events; limit debris. Territory is
understandable. Co-op attacks need committed targets. Rewards enable new
actions. Renewable resources and progression coexist.

Start with Old Copper: a ruined collector, repairable rods, movable
stormstones, loose terrain, a village that disagrees about the solution.
