1. CRITICAL ASSESSMENT

The dungeon reads as patterned blocks and encounter objects inside a dark box. The room envelope, construction and purpose are weakly expressed. Bright repeated motifs carry more visual weight than entrances, threat silhouettes or the principal objective.

Architecture:
- Large floor and ceiling planes have little internal hierarchy. The visible ceiling is a brown slab with irregular downward blocks and repeated purple clusters.
- Supports, changes in span, circulation and damage do not form a clear construction language in these views.
- Several images provide little visual information about the far wall, exits or overall volume.
- Scattered steps, shelves and platforms do not by themselves establish meaningful elevation or route choice.
- The solution is a coherent structure with deliberate circulation and focal points, followed by selective detail.

Ceiling:
- Similar purple star clusters repeat at similar importance, creating a ceiling-wide decorative field.
- Their suspension is not visually explained by a containment device, support or framed opening.
- Their brightness draws attention upward without reliably helping the player understand the room.
- A ceiling needs bays, beams/ribs, piers, recesses and exceptional focal features. It should vary with room role.

Materials:
- The cyan and red repeated cross patterns read like printed wrapping paper. They repeat over broad faces, thin strips and disconnected shapes without describing a clear substance.
- Purple star patterns occur so frequently that a potentially special material loses significance.
- Quiet base surfaces, structural divisions, restrained state marks and localized wear would provide more identity than increased texture resolution or more random noise.
- Preserve cosmic materials in important places. Do not erase the intended magical identity.

Lighting:
- The foreground is readable, while large enclosure surfaces disappear. Bright small objects often do not communicate the volume around them.
- Determine whether this results from light placement, missing/weak local lighting, shadow/ambient behavior, exposure or geometry. Do not assume that every bright texture is a working light emitter.
- Reveal selected doorway edges, wall planes, ribs and vertical connectors with authored practical light. Preserve dark recesses and atmosphere.
- A global ambient boost would flatten the room. Unlimited dynamic lights and bloom would add cost and visual interference.

Focal object:
- The Undertow Knot resembles a luminous wireframe tent on stacked blocks.
- Give it an intentional base, containment frame, connection to the room and visible state language.
- Locked, powered, disrupted and claimed states should be understandable on the object. Text supplies detail rather than the entire meaning.
- The visible instruction proves one combat gate. It does not prove every dungeon objective is a kill-all gate.

Enemies:
- The Warden has a broad tan slab body, weak head definition, thin legs, a simple weapon and a flat dark shield. Its shape poorly explains mass, joints, protection and attack direction. A thin overhead strip is also visible; verify whether it is model geometry or world-space UI.
- The crab-like creature reads as horizontal rails on thin legs, with limited shell volume or distinction between working claws and locomotion.
- Small airborne forms are difficult to distinguish from pickups or particles.
- Verify the actual model-to-archetype mapping. The large crystal/root form must not silently become an invented fourth enemy.
- Screenshots cannot prove broken AI. Require motion evidence for behavioral criticism.

UI:
- The pause screen is comparatively coherent and readable. Its thematic frame can be improved by the existing UI owner.
- Target panels and the Knot instruction compete with the world, while the corresponding objects need stronger visual identity.
- Retain the requested pixel-perfect gold/silver bookbinding and magitech direction. Avoid launching a second HUD redesign during the architectural work.

2. QUALITY BENCHMARKS

Noita:
Its official description connects spells to materials, including burning, melting, freezing and other simulated interactions. The transferable requirement is that environmental changes alter available actions. A fire particle effect with no effect on the surrounding material does not establish this quality.

Minecraft:
Mojang's Warden design account describes a deliberately nontraditional threat centered on avoidance, stealth and player-caused escalation. The transferable requirement is an enemy whose rules are legible and whose presence changes navigation and choices. Our Sanctum Warden should remain an original design with its own role.

FTL:
Subset's description presents several responses to the same system failure, including power redistribution, escape, increased attack and boarding. The transferable requirement is a setback that changes decisions and resource costs. A dungeon should support adaptation instead of routinely demanding a prescribed action sequence.

Factorio:
The developers describe designing enemy traversal and pollution-source targeting to change defensive choices, while extending and optimizing existing movement systems. The transferable requirement is a visible relationship between infrastructure, enemy behavior and player decisions.

These are design lessons. None of the sources establishes that those games use the exact dungeon-generation method proposed here. Overall parity or superiority requires repeated play, content breadth, stability and performance evidence beyond one screenshot or one encounter.

3. ART DIRECTION: REBUILD THE EXISTING ASTRAL SANCTUM

Interpret the place as an underground astronomical archive and working magitech institution that has been damaged, altered and partly occupied.

Retain current in-game names and IDs unless a deliberate content migration is needed. "Astral Sanctum" is the direction for this pass, not a request to rename the game or add another biome.

Material roles:
- Primary mass: restrained warm-gray or violet-gray stone, with broad quiet areas.
- Secondary mass: dark structural metal and selected worn ivory/stone plates.
- Important fittings: aged gold/brass clasps and silver guides, rims and precision parts.
- Utility lighting: warm amber lamps mounted to usable circulation and work areas.
- Active equipment: limited cool indications with recognizable shape and motion.
- Astral feature: concentrated violet star-glass in one aperture, contained floor, device or recess.
- Wear: soot at heat sources, deposits below leaks, polish along contact surfaces, fractures following construction and damage.

Gold signals selected importance; it should not cover every wall. Silver makes connections and working parts legible. The UI and environment should share this language without making every architectural feature resemble a literal book.

Texture requirements:
- Consistent world-space texel density across related assets.
- Material-specific pattern scale and orientation.
- Structural divisions at a larger scale than pixel detail.
- Sparse symbols with meaning.
- Filtered distant detail and stable normal/roughness response.
- No identical tiny cross applied to every face as the primary material identity.
- Avoid baking directional illumination into base color.

Composition:
- One dominant spatial form per room.
- One primary focal object or destination.
- Useful subordinate landmarks.
- Quiet surfaces around threats and interactables.
- Deliberate compression before a major reveal.
- Visible relationships between floor levels, exits and ceiling height.

4. ROOM AND CEILING KIT

Build a small reusable construction kit over the current voxel generator:
- Plain wall bay with clear top and bottom termination.
- Pier/capital and supported beam.
- Doorway/lintel and service opening.
- Stair/landing and balcony/railing.
- Maintenance trench and appropriate cover.
- Straight ceiling/coffer.
- Stepped vault rib and infill.
- Framed oculus or suspended-apparatus mount.
- Existing-material damage variants.
- Purposeful transition between constructed stone and natural cave.

Select bay dimensions in physical world units, then quantize to the supported voxel scale. Use actual player and enemy clearance profiles. A visually broad Warden must fit its assigned approach. A decorative beam must not silently remove required headroom.

Ceiling choices follow room roles:

Service vestibule
- Compressed height, supported beams or shallow coffers.
- Service lines lead to equipment.
- Warm light establishes safe first footing and an intelligible route.

Gallery
- Repeated ribs ending at wall piers, interrupted by alcoves and overlooks.
- A visible destination through the gallery.
- Height and cover create a distinct tactical situation.

Workshop
- Taller central bay with a balcony and meaningful machinery.
- Hoists/rails or restraints have attachment points.
- Maintenance access connects to the device it serves.

Binding chamber
- Monumental stepped/corbel vault.
- One framed star-glass aperture or contained suspended resonator.
- Quiet surrounding mass increases the feature's importance.
- The ceiling composition leads toward the objective.

Phantom-floor chamber
- Preserve the intended puzzle/traversal rule.
- Use a small number of readable route groups and meaningful gaps.
- Indicate stable, changing and absent states using shape, value, motion, sound and optional symbols; do not rely only on color.
- Provide a preview or safe demonstration of the rule.
- Give unintended falls a credible recovery route where this encounter's design permits it.
- A jump that visually reads as possible must agree with collision and supported movement.

Natural breach
- A deliberately irregular opening breaks the institutional structure.
- Debris follows a plausible source, water marks have an origin, and light/sound communicate the connection.
- This supplies contrast without making every room noisy.

Suspended magical objects can remain. Frame their suspension through restraints, pylons, an energized aperture or another legible mechanism. Use a few selected objects; do not make decorative scatter the entire ceiling system.

Decorative pieces can be static meshed/instanced geometry. Anything presented as walkable, blocking, destructible or mechanically active must use authoritative collision/state. Full dynamic structural collapse is not a prerequisite; unsupported decorative debris should not claim physical behavior that does not exist.

5. THREE ENEMY ROLES

FIRST: SANCTUM WARDEN — LANE CONTROL

Visual construction:
A compact stone/ivory reliquary body over a dark articulated frame. Layered plates, a recessed head/core, broad knees and substantial feet. A thick stepped shield with binding-clasp corner mounts and a visible forearm/grip. Verify whether the existing overhead strip belongs to the model or to world-space UI. A crown or crest is an optional new design choice; do not interpret an interface element as existing anatomy. Keep its defensive side obvious.

Behavior:
A deliberate shield-leading approach, planted brace, committed short bash and exposed recovery. It holds a useful lane rather than converting its shield into omnidirectional damage reduction.

Counterplay:
Change angle, bait the committed attack, use cover, exploit the exposed flank/core during recovery, or alter terrain. Early tracking can be limited; committed direction and active damage interval must be readable.

Rules:
The oriented shield blocks through its actual pose and material. A hit on the back is not a front-shield hit. Projectiles meet a consistent collision rule; friendly or hostile ownership should not silently make matter intangible. Any deliberate team exception needs an explicit design rationale.

Reward proposal:
Recover a shield component that becomes usable cover through existing equipment/placement systems. Reuse the same meaningful blocking behavior rather than creating an unrelated percentage bonus. Give placement an honest cost and persistent item identity.

SECOND: AETHER MOTH — AERIAL PRESSURE AND DISTRACTION

Visual construction:
Recognizable paired angular wings, a compact thorax, abdomen and antennae. Use broad planes and negative space. Concentrate emission in a small core/lens. The outline must read as a creature before it reads as a pickup.

Behavior:
Transit, braking hover, visible preparation, limited aimed burst/dart and recovery/reposition. Body banking and wing pose communicate the action. Keep it distinguishable from the Warden in movement and attack geometry.

Counterplay:
Break line of sight during preparation, exploit the open pose, use ceiling clearance, or influence attention through supported light/noise stimuli.

Rules:
Light seeking queries logical emitter/stimulus data. It does not read screen brightness, bloom or exposure. Search locally with bounded work. Physical walls block its route/projectiles. A sealed compartment should not receive an unexplained attack from an omniscient flyer.

If regeneration remains, expose an interruptible source or action. The screenshot's REGENERATING label does not establish how it currently works.

Reward proposal:
A recovered lens becomes a usable light/lure through the same emitter/stimulus system. Establish one working use rather than adding an unused crafting ingredient.

THIRD: EXISTING CRAB-LIKE ARCHETYPE — LOW FLANKING PRESSURE

Visual construction:
An arched layered shell, thick leg roots, clear joints, planted tips and recognizable working claws. Preserve a contrasting sensor and accessible face/underside. Use a low broad silhouette distinct from upright Warden and airborne moth.

Behavior:
Lateral movement, planted claw preparation, a short committed lunge and recovery. It seeks useful flanking space.

Counterplay:
Bait the lunge, exploit recovery, use hard cover, change elevation or alter its support. Its shell protection and exposed region should be spatially understandable.

Optional extension within the existing damage system:
A bounded local chipping action can open a weak obstacle. Respect material hardness, per-action work, local route feasibility and persistent edits. This must not become unrestricted tunneling toward the player. If the current terrain-damage operation cannot support it coherently, finish the core flanking encounter first and report the dependency.

Reward proposal:
A mandible/tool part supplies the same limited chipping capability through existing tools/augments. Complete one acquisition-to-use loop.

If chipping is deferred, defer its player-tool reward as well. Use an existing functional reward for that iteration and report the missing capability. Do not implement a player-only imitation while claiming it reuses the creature's action.

Shared enemy standards:
- Simulation owns wind-up, commitment, active hit interval, recovery and interruption.
- Rendering interpolates authoritative state.
- Evaluated pose informs hit regions and weapon/shield geometry.
- Evaluate action-driven weapon, shield and hit-region transforms on simulation ticks. Rendering interpolates those same poses; simulation never reads render-interpolated transforms back. Sweep fast geometry between previous and current authoritative poses.
- Use a few meaningful collision volumes, not collision for every decorative voxel.
- Swept movement/projectiles prevent fast actions tunneling through newly placed walls.
- Footing, stairs and ledges agree with the visible body.
- Reuse rigid-part hierarchy and existing animation/entity systems; no new heavyweight engine or mandatory skinning pipeline.
- Prepare geometry once and update transforms/instances. Avoid per-frame mesh rebuilding.
- Use distinct animation and spatial sound cues, with visual equivalents for critical information.
- Test each alone before combining them.

Terrain edits must reach movement/projectile collision through authoritative occupancy, an immediate local collision patch or a coherent collider publication step. Marking a navigation path dirty is insufficient if the next movement check still uses obsolete collision.

Reward pickup and placement transfer one persistent item instance between world and inventory ownership. Resolve death/drop/claim once across reload; do not create a second instance during the transfer.

Pairings:
- Warden + moth: lane control plus exposed-peek pressure; physical cover remains useful.
- Warden + crab: front pressure and a changing flank.
- Moth + crab: vertical attention plus low approach; off-screen cues become important.

Begin with modest encounter density. More enemies, simultaneous unavoidable attacks and larger health bars are not substitutes for better decisions.

6. A COMPLETE FIRST SITE

Use the existing expedition/site system. The following is a proposed reference arrangement, not a new quest framework or a requirement to rebuild all generated dungeons.

Entrance overlook:
The player sees the principal apparatus and can inspect a threat before committing. Provide safe first footing and understandable retreat.

Upper gallery:
A shorter exposed approach with the Warden controlling a useful lane. Piers provide real cover. Its destination remains visible intermittently.

Service works:
A longer lower route that reveals the apparatus's actual connections. It offers an existing-system interaction, a material opportunity or a resource cost distinct from the upper route.

Binding chamber:
The redesigned Knot has an authored base and clear state. The main objective is visible in the world. Spatial circulation allows more than shooting straight ahead.

Recovery route:
Connect the lower part of a traversal/puzzle chamber back into the site. It provides a consequence and altered route after a mistake, rather than automatic objective failure.

Return connector:
A lift, stair, repaired bridge or opened passage creates a shorter return route and a new view of the entrance. Use a connector the current engine can implement fully.

Proposed connectivity:
- Entrance reaches upper gallery and service works.
- Both routes reach the binding chamber through different situations.
- The lower recovery route reconnects to service works.
- A changed/activated return connector links the objective area to the entrance.
- Actual digging, building and destruction may create other routes.

Use these relationships, not this exact shape, to vary seeds. A mirrored room with different props is not sufficient variation. Vary approach elevation, observation angle, route cost, threat placement, system state and shortcut location while preserving coherent construction.

The graph describes initial intent. Runtime voxel geometry and collision remain authoritative after player changes.

7. FUNCTIONAL CHOICES AND CONSEQUENCES

Choose a few interactions already supported by the current game, then complete them thoroughly.

A suitable first power-based situation:
- The containment apparatus, work lights and an existing service mechanism have visible connections.
- Isolating a shared supply can disable containment while also disabling the connected lights/mechanism.
- That changes access and retreat costs. Emergency/readability lighting and a viable physical way out remain.
- Restoring an alternate supply or taking the longer route are possible responses when supported.
- Each effect follows the actual power graph and state; a painted cable plus a scripted room flag does not satisfy the requirement.

A terrain-based situation:
- A weak partition, destructible support or buildable gap provides an alternate approach.
- Mining/building uses the same material/tool operations as elsewhere.
- Costs follow visible rules: time, tools, noise, material expenditure or a changed encounter.
- Accept a legitimate bypass. Do not turn every wall indestructible or add a punishment whenever the player avoids a planned fight.

A creature-based situation:
- A real light/lure influences the moth.
- An enemy's physical shield can become cover.
- A limited chipping action changes a local route.
- These capabilities also work outside the specific demonstration room where their shared rules allow it.

Use water, heat, combustion or other reactions only through implemented authoritative systems. If such a system is missing, do not substitute particles and claim systemic behavior, or launch a new fluid/chemistry engine as a prerequisite to this dungeon pass.

Outcome policy:
- Explicitly declare whether the reference site's alternative approach changes physical access, changes how guardians are defeated, or satisfies a separate environmental objective. Knot appearance, instruction text, unsealing and claim eligibility must agree. A legacy guardian-gated reward retains that requirement; a noncombat outcome uses an explicit supported objective configuration.
- Discovery rewards, combat objectives and environmental objectives have distinct eligibility.
- Preserve valid legacy sanctum claims and guardian membership.
- An alternate objective route must not fabricate guardian deaths.
- A killed guardian falling into a pit still resolves through its stable identity; unloading is not death.
- Persist claimed rewards, terrain changes, mechanism state and relevant encounter state.
- When a rare reward object is destroyed or its location becomes inaccessible, use the established recovery/claim policy rather than duplicating it or silently losing it.
- Ordinary traversal and exploration should not require every nearby enemy to die.

FTL-like pressure comes from connected constraints and recoverable setbacks. Define the state change and new choices after an alarm, broken machine, failed jump or lost resource. Reserve hard failure for explicit rules communicated beforehand.

PROMPT 2 — REBUILD THREE ENEMIES AS DISTINCT PHYSICAL AND TACTICAL DESIGNS

Act as creature artist, animator and combat designer. Identify the actual Warden, Aether Moth and crab-like archetype before editing. Do not assume the large crystal/root form is the moth.

Rebuild the Warden around a planted, asymmetrical shield-bearing silhouette; the moth around unmistakable articulated wings and a small luminous core; and the crab around an arched shell, working claws and clear low locomotion.

Use the detailed roles in Section 5:
- Warden: lane control, brace, committed bash, exposed recovery.
- Moth: aerial positioning, visible preparation, limited attack and reposition; supported light/stimulus manipulation.
- Crab: low flanking pressure, committed lunge and honest recovery; optional bounded chipping through existing terrain damage.

For each, complete locomotion, orientation, preparation, commitment, hit, miss, interruption where supported, recovery, damage and death. Pose and sound should communicate intent at normal combat distance.

Simulation owns action timing and damage. Make the rendered pose, shield/weapon transforms and hit regions agree. Empty space between appendages is not an invisible solid box. Use swept collision for fast motion and verify attacks against walls placed during preparation.

Respect actual clearance, footing and current terrain. Invalidate affected navigation locally after edits, validate the next movement segment and bound replanning. If a path disappears, use an honest supported behavior: guard, seek another route, retreat, fall or perform the defined excavation. Do not teleport or phase to hide path failures.

Use local logical stimulus queries rather than rendered brightness for moth attention. If regeneration/toxicity remains, make its source, application and counterplay understandable without a giant status panel.

Finish one useful recovered capability per creature through current item/tool/placement systems where those paths exist: shield cover, light/lure lens, limited chipping tool. Avoid unused drops and speculative recipe trees.

Reuse shared geometry/material resources and a small rigid-part pose hierarchy. Do not rebuild meshes per frame or require a new skinning/behavior-tree framework.

Prove each enemy alone, then each relevant pair, in rooms that support its role. Show silhouette and neutral/preparation/recovery poses at gameplay distance, plus short real combat recordings including missed attacks and terrain changes. Profile actual encounter and declared stress populations.

Use bounded generation retries and report failures. Repair or reject bad candidates rather than hiding unreachable content with teleportation.

9. ACCEPTANCE SCORECARD

Architecture and ceiling:
- A doorway view communicates the main volume, route, focal object and likely exits.
- Upward views show construction, rhythm and one or a few special features.
- Large surfaces remain quiet enough for threats and interactables to stand out.
- Supports and magical suspension have visible logic.

Enemies:
- Warden, moth and crab are recognizable by silhouette and movement before reading a nameplate.
- Their threats demand different responses.
- Preparation, commitment and recovery are readable.
- Collision, protection, footing and terrain interaction match visible construction.
- Their recovered capabilities have at least one completed useful application where included.

Layouts:
- The first site includes a meaningful loop/alternative, useful elevation, observation before commitment and a return connection.
- Generated candidates pass actual geometry/clearance checks.
- Seed variation changes navigation or decisions, not just ornament.
- Legitimate digging/building remains useful.

Systemic play:
- The same outcome can be reached through two substantially different approaches in the reference site.
- One mistake produces an altered but playable situation.
- Machinery and materials change actual shared state.
- Legacy claims and guardian identities remain correct.
- Edits and rewards persist without duplication.

Presentation and performance:
- Lighting shows chosen depth cues without uniformly washing the room.
- Phantom-floor states are understandable and agree with collision.
- Critical cues survive reduced effects and color-independent inspection.
- Runtime captures establish the result; generated concept art is not evidence.
- Performance is measured with the integrated scene and active gameplay systems.

A useful first playtest task is: enter without directions, identify the objective and an exit, explain the threat, choose an approach, recover from one setback, and use a reward in the next situation. Record where the player hesitates or misunderstands. Do not infer fun or comprehension from test counts alone.
