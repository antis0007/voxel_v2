WORLD SHADER UPGRADE — INTEGRATION AND DESIGN NOTES
2026-10-19

DELIVERABLE
===========
world_shader.wgsl is the complete revised shader body. Replace the supplied
world_shader.wgsl body at its existing location and assemble it with the same
real shared WGSL declarations and functions that your renderer already uses.
The package also includes an exact unified diff, the original body, and small
reproducible validation fixtures. Do not install the validation stubs in the
application: they are deliberately simplified substitutes for missing includes.

The upload is a shader body, not a complete standalone WGSL module. Camera,
lighting, shadow bindings, atmosphere, cloud-layer and underwater functions
are referenced but not declared in it. No renderer source, terrain meshes,
screenshots, videos, actual shared includes, or GPU timings were provided.

This revision fixes demonstrated shader faults and introduces more consistent
material and lighting approximations. It is not a certification of flawless
landscape rendering. It has not been integrated or viewed in the actual game.

COMPATIBILITY AND MATERIAL CALIBRATION
=====================================
- Vertex attributes remain locations 0–4 with the supplied formats. No new
  texture, sampler, uniform buffer, storage buffer, or bind group is required.
- vs_main and fs_main keep their names; both color and receiver outputs remain.
  Main-camera depth remains reverse-Z. Shadow comparisons retain the original
  forward-Z direction. The renderer must keep its corresponding depth states.
- VsOut location 1 now carries camera-relative position instead of absolute
  world position. Both stages in this file were updated together. New internal
  location 9 carries four interpolated material-coverage weights. The stage
  interface totals 10 locations and 31 scalar components. These are internal
  varyings, not additional host vertex attributes.
- Base vertex colors are assumed sRGB, matching the original pow(color, 2.2).
  The exact sRGB transfer is now applied per vertex, before interpolation.
  Lighting and both render targets remain linear HDR. Do not tone-map or
  gamma-encode this pass; the shared HDR resolve owns that transformation.
- Material tints retain their existing multiplicative interpretation. Natural
  climate fields use actual voxel/world coordinates, so changing material
  texture scale no longer moves beaches, snow altitude, or climate. The
  material scale still affects manufactured surfaces. A separate natural
  microtexture-scale control would need an explicit additional convention.
- Material reflectance now uses the conventional dielectric parameter mapping
  F0 = 0.16 * reflectance^2: reflectance 0.5 yields F0 0.04. The original used
  reflectance as an additional empirical gain, so existing presets may need
  recalibration. If your host already stores physical F0, change dielectric_f0
  to vec3<f32>(reflectance), rather than applying the squared mapping twice.
- Existing metal families 47–51 use albedo as conductor F0 and suppress diffuse.
  Water uses F0 0.0204. Water receiver alpha remains negative, but its magnitude
  now agrees with water Fresnel and foam. Check that the reflection resolve
  replaces/blends its sky fallback rather than adding the same reflection twice.
- Removing empirical specular multipliers intentionally changes appearance.
  Compare using the actual exposure, sky, light intensities and materials.
  The shader cannot determine their intended calibration from this attachment.

DISTANT TERRAIN AND MATERIAL CHANGES
===================================
1. Stable detail across distance and resolution

The original far_shade selected wavelengths from floor(log2(footprint)), but
its adjacent octaves used different random offsets. A smooth blend within
one octave therefore still jumped when the octave index changed. The revision
uses fixed 48 m and 12 m world-space relief fields. Their amplitudes diminish
as they become unresolved; the pattern itself never changes frequency or phase
in response to camera distance. The same field shades resident natural terrain
and its distant approximation.

The screen footprint is the largest singular value of the surface derivative
matrix. It covers diagonal/sheared pixel stretch that the original maximum of
two derivative lengths could underestimate. Noise uses smooth quintic
reconstruction with a matching analytic gradient. Relief is applied within the
normal's tangent plane so a cliff is not bent toward world up.

Grain, clumps, mottling, scree, bedding, snow-drift variation, sand ripples,
foliage and several manufactured-material patterns now attenuate toward
representative means. This follows procedural octave-clamping practice [1].
It is conservative minification, not an exact convolution of every nonlinear
material mask. In particular, the snow and geological masks remain authored
approximations; noise does not reconstruct actual erosion or concavity.

2. Shared material coverage

The vertex stage decodes the existing packed material key into turf, soil,
rock and sand coverage weights. Those weights interpolate across triangles;
material IDs do not. The fragment stage evaluates climate once, then combines
only the contributing material responses. Resident natural materials use the
same evaluator. Unknown/noncovered far materials retain authored base color.
Kind 16 keeps its resident special treatment and falls back to authored color
in the distant mesh; matching every other custom material would require more
coverage channels or a renderer-provided material representation.

This removes the old forced grass treatment and fixed 42% cover blend for
ordinary distant terrain. It also removes the sudden foliage palette branch
at a 0.5 m pixel footprint, which mistakenly treated wood as canopy. Geometric
normals control slope-dependent material coverage; relief normals only change
lighting afterward. Actual near/far geometric normals still must agree where
the mesher intends a seamless transition.

3. Correct geometry classes

Procedural backdrop uses -1.5 < ao_strength < 0. Exact edited fallback uses
ao_strength < -1.5. Exact fallback is no longer sunk/discarded as a procedural
heightfield, and it receives the same real cascaded shadows as resident mesh.
Only the solid terrain proxy receives the existing compatibility underlap;
water and foliage proxies are no longer lowered with it.

The resident/proxy radial coverage contract and the broad underlap are still
present because the supplied interface has no actual residency mask or parent
height data. Removing their assumptions safely requires the mesh/streaming
implementation. Values exactly at -1.5 were not assigned a new meaning.

LIGHTING, SHADOWS, WATER AND ATMOSPHERE
======================================
The specular BRDF now uses GGX with height-correlated Smith visibility and
Schlick Fresnel, with perceptual roughness remapping [2]. Diffuse and specular
share the same finalized material state. The shader no longer separately
suppresses reflection with (1-roughness)^2. Geometric normal variance and an
explicit approximate allowance for filtered-away slopes widen the specular
lobe before it becomes unstable. This is not a measured slope distribution.

Metals receive a bounded sky-environment approximation. A real prefiltered
environment, BRDF integration LUT and scene reflections remain renderer work;
one reflected sky lookup is not full physically accurate image-based lighting.
Existing authored local-light attenuation is retained, and local lights still
lack shadow maps. Existing atmospheric and artistic fill terms also remain
approximations rather than a full global-illumination solution.

Shadow sampling uses explicit-level comparison, receiver-plane depth
correction, full filter-footprint border checks, and smooth cascade/projection
coverage. It can continue through every eligible coarser cascade, rather than
trying only one. This follows established CSM filtering considerations [3].
Orthographic light matrices with homogeneous w=1 are required. Missing casters,
light-space depth coverage and texel-stable cascade positioning must be fixed
in the renderer. The same pass cannot reconstruct their missing information.

The cloud field represents low/mid/high layer optical depths, not RGB spectra.
Its accumulated depth is nonnegative, cloud attenuation fades continuously
near the horizon, and moon lighting samples the moon's projection. Clip levels
with zero weight no longer issue their four manual bilinear texture loads.

Distant water no longer inherits the land roughness floor of 0.62. Filtered
wave/bed/foam detail and a smooth fade of resident-only optics reduce the
material handoff. Water now has one direct GGX reflection contribution,
Fresnel-weighted environment reflection and body lighting. Duplicate glint
lobes and a shadow-independent sunlight floor are removed. Vertical reflection
azimuths are safely normalized. Water scattering follows solar intensity.

The existing wave shape, bathymetry encoding, refraction, caustics and shoreline
models remain approximations. They do not become a full fluid simulation or
screen-space refraction merely because the BRDF is improved. Matching near/far
water depths and mesh edges still requires coherent terrain data. The existing
vertex wave attenuation also needs shared edge sampling across unequal mesh
sizes if those edges can coincide while displaced.

Atmosphere is composed directly as L = S + T*L_surface, where shared RGB is
in-scattered radiance and shared alpha is transmittance. The original code
strongly implies this contract; confirm it in aerial_perspective and local_mist.
The revision avoids division by tiny extinction and compositing cloud opacity
through that unstable recovered color. Cloud fog has a separate source term.
Legacy height fog integrates a capped exponential density along the ray,
including horizontal and layer-crossing rays. The extra kilometre haze and
terrain-only post-fog night grade are removed so they do not alter sky radiance
a second time. Streaming fade remains a separate outer-coverage approximation.

Check whether atmosphere_color includes explicit sun/moon disks. If it does,
exclude those disks from specular environment evaluation or account for their
energy consistently with analytic direct lighting. Shared fog/cloud passes must
also establish a single owner for each medium; otherwise duplicate integration
can remain outside this shader. The homogeneous cloud-layer fog here cannot
recover actual cloud density or scattering order along an arbitrary view ray.

PERFORMANCE AND REMAINING ENGINE WORK
=====================================
The shader skips fully filtered noise and zero-weight weather clip levels.
Material coverage shares one climate evaluation. Shadow filtering normally
performs nine comparisons, often eighteen in transition regions, with up to
thirty-six at coincident projection-edge transitions. Full GGX, extra sky
reflection lookup and broader shadow receiver coverage can add work. Measure
GPU pass timestamps in the actual renderer before choosing quality settings.

To reach a dependable distant-terrain result, prioritize:

1. Matching parent/child boundary geometry and normals with an explicit morph
   or stitch, using a shared terrain source. Geometry clipmaps demonstrate
   coordinated geometric transitions [4]; arbitrary depth sinking cannot
   guarantee identical silhouettes.
2. Actual residency coverage: every visible sample needs a resident mesh or a
   valid proxy while chunks load. Edited regions, caves and overhangs need
   explicit proxy exclusion or a representation that contains their geometry.
3. Consistent distant material coverage, heights and low-frequency normals from
   the same terrain data, including edits. Consider a mipmapped terrain cache
   with material weights and normal/slope variance when shader evaluation cost
   or remaining nonlinear minification artifacts justify it.
4. Distant shadow casters/horizon occlusion, stabilized CSM placement and valid
   caster depth bounds. Verify dawn/dusk, cascade edges, moving clouds and terrain
   that extends beyond the last cascade.
5. The actual HDR/atmosphere/water resolve and temporal antialiasing: verify
   motion vectors for deformed water, disocclusion, camera cuts, exposure changes,
   grazing views, wet terrain and residency changes during fast flight.

The files needed for that next integration pass are the shared WGSL include
assembly, camera/light/shadow declarations, terrain/LOD mesher, residency/streaming
selection, shadow-caster setup, and final HDR/atmosphere/water resolve, together
with a short moving-camera capture showing the current defects.
