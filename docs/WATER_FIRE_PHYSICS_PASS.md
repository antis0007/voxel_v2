# Water, splash and fire physics pass

This pass targets two separate problems: frame-time spikes from terrain physics,
and the visible sluggishness of water/fire presentation. It does **not** turn
the voxel sandbox into a full Navier–Stokes, SPH or pressure-projection engine.

## Water transaction fast path

The cellular solver previously committed each side of a water move through
`World::set_water_volume`. For one transfer that meant:

- two `set_many` transactions;
- two to four revision increments;
- repeated snapshot invalidation;
- duplicate ten-chunk wake sweeps;
- repeated resident-edit and water-volume lookups.

`World::apply_water_pair` now commits both cells atomically. Intra-chunk
transfers take a dedicated path with one chunk borrow, one dirty mark, one
snapshot invalidation, one revision bump and one paired wake sweep. Cross-chunk
transfers use a two-cell form of the same transaction. If the second half is
refused (missing/corrupt resident edit), the first half is restored to its
pre-transfer volume.

The transfer rule also now moves up to four units per face when equalising.
That does not add transfer attempts; it lets a breach reach a stable level in
fewer simulation ticks. Hydraulic outlet searches still stagger and still move
a bounded packet.

Chunk-admission probes are deduplicated before `Activity::can_wake`, so adjacent
cells usually test one or two chunks instead of twenty-two raw coordinates.

Verification includes a two-cell transaction microbenchmark (ignored by
default), mass-conservation tests, a single-revision assertion and the existing
pressure/ledge tests.

## Shared simulation frame deadline

Factory machines, rigid assemblies and water used independent budgets
(approximately 1 ms + 0.8 ms + 2.5 ms). They now share one 2.5 ms update
deadline. Factory work gets at most 750 µs and rigid bodies at most 600 µs,
with the remaining time going to the near/fair water lanes. A long machine or
tree-body update can no longer push the whole scheduler several milliseconds
past the frame target.

## Thermal storage

Sparse thermal cells moved from `BTreeMap` to `HashMap`. The heat lattice
performs several lookups per burning cell per tick; hash lookup removes the
logarithmic ordering cost from that hot path. Save encoding sorts keys, so the
serialized bytes remain deterministic. Burn positions are also indexed in a
bounded runtime `HashSet` for local lights.

## Fire

- Fire shading now uses a vertically advected, world-anchored noise field with
  a black-body ramp: deep orange edges, yellow tongues and a white-hot core.
- Smoke and steam silhouettes breathe instead of using one fixed circular
  cutout.
- Burning cells feed a bounded local-light list. Fire now illuminates adjacent
  cave walls; stale entries are validated against the world before upload.
- Water contact quenches fire into ash and emits a short steam puff at the
  open cell above it.

## Splashes

Water entry now produces:

- a bounded expanding contact ring with an inner trough and eight crown jets;
- white/teal droplet voxels with gravity and short lifetimes;
- a procedural `Splash` cue;
- a pool of at most 24 rings.

Contact height comes from `World::water_surface_at`, which walks the local
water column using the true partial volume. Roofed water and deep interior
points are rejected rather than producing a ring on the wrong plane.

Events are generated by:

- the player entering or leaving water, using the pre-drag vertical speed;
- the first surface crossing of a projectile;
- a rigid assembly's first submerged tick, scaled by downward speed.

## Momentum, basins and sloshing

Water cells now carry a tiny runtime-only horizontal velocity in volume units
per simulation tick. It is created by a transfer, biased into the next
equalisation pass, and damped every active tick.

- A pulse can overshoot a level basin and return instead of stopping at the
  first equal height.
- Momentum wakes its cell until it decays, so a wave is not put to sleep after
  one scheduler tick.
- The field is bounded to 65,536 cells and is not serialized. It represents a
  transient wave, not authoritative mass.
- Removing or editing water clears its motion; mass conservation is unchanged.
- The partial-volume mesh already renders real surface heights, so this makes
  basin filling animate as a moving level rather than a static plane.

## Beaches, surf and swash

Generator 22 adds a new coast profile without changing older saves:

- wider low-angle surf platforms;
- offshore bars and troughs;
- a lower berm, swale and dune toe;
- an extended sand limit behind the beach;
- animated wet-sand run-up and cusp shading.

The water vertex stage now uses linear shoaling: wave displacement grows as the
bed shallows instead of being suppressed there. Slow set envelopes lift
occasional breaking crests. The fragment stage adds moving depth-contour break
fronts, stronger swash lace, run-up variation and whitecaps. The swash line on
sand breathes with the same time base, so the wet band and the water read as one
coastal event.

These are still bounded procedural responses. They do not run a shallow-water
wave solver on the CPU mesh; the voxel volume field remains the authority for
mass and collision.

## Scope

The solver remains a conservative, quantised voxel volume model with
hydrostatic outlet searches, not an incompressible continuum solver. Splashes
are a visual surface response, not an additional mass source. There is no SPH,
vorticity confinement, wave-equation pressure projection or GPU readback loop in
this pass. Subjective in-game capture and a before/after FPS benchmark remain
required; only source checks, unit tests and the release test build were run
here.
