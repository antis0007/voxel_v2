# Hydrology and evolving rivers

Literature and implementation review through 6 November 2026. The objective is to reproduce measurable behavior from headwaters to the coast, then generate plausible new rivers with the same processes. The landscape model, hydraulic model, and evolving-bed model each need their own evidence.

## Recent results worth using

| Research | Evidence and application to AXIOM |
| --- | --- |
| **Song et al., October 2026. Unsteady discharge and lake deltas.** | The authors derived synthetic flood sequences from Ganjiang River records and ran 256 idealized Delft3D simulations. Stronger peaks increased lateral growth, overbank deposition, and bifurcation under their inlet sediment rule. The model files and boundary data are public. Reproduce contrasting runs to check that changing the flow and sediment history changes the *kind* of delta, then treat ocean forcing as a separate experiment. [Paper and data statement](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JF009007) |
| **Zhang et al., September 2026. Waves and mouth bars.** | Coupled Delft3D FLOW and WAVE experiments produced different bar shapes and cases with no bar. Strong waves or closely spaced outlets could inhibit formation under the conditions studied. The public setup includes the grid, bathymetry, boundary conditions, and wave forcing. This is a reproducible process experiment with idealized geometry. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JF008259) · [Model files](https://zenodo.org/records/21694881) |
| **Sadana et al., September 2026. SFINCS flood hindcasts.** | In a 499-event evaluation against satellite flood maps, replacing modeled inflow with observed discharge raised flood extent overlap (CSI) from 0.39 to 0.67 in ten US events. A separate six-event comparison improved from 0.37 to 0.57 with roughly 1 m rather than 30 m terrain input. This evaluates flood extent over a fixed bed. Subgrid storage and conveyance are promising for broad floodplains, and measured inflow and bathymetry are priorities. [Paper and assets](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-5491-2026) |
| **Jover et al., September 2026. SWOT discharge.** | Across 569 benchmarked South American reaches, median Kling–Gupta efficiency was 0.43 for the consensus discharge product. Use remotely sensed water elevation, width, and slope as additional constraints on large rivers. Retain gauged discharge for validation where available. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026GL122871) |
| **McDonald and Cordonnier, SIGGRAPH 2026. Stochastic geomorphological transport.** | A parallel point-based transport method evolves water and sediment with momentum. The paper demonstrates emerging meanders, braids, and deltas, plus a momentum ablation and a resolution comparison. It is a direct alternative to the Schott terrain-amplification approach for a *world-generation* prototype. Evaluate it for landform distributions and runtime, and establish hydraulic and sediment accuracy independently. [Paper and demonstrations](https://erosiv.studio/publications/stochastic-geomorphological-transport) · [Author implementation](https://github.com/erosiv/geotransport) |
| **Prasojo et al., May 2026. Flood intermittency in pyDeltaRCM.** | Delta extent and channel count responded non-monotonically to the frequency of large events in a numerical ensemble. Use a repeatable sequence of normal flows and floods. Compare the distribution of outcomes from multiple histories. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025GL120954) · [Model implementation](https://github.com/DeltaRCM/pyDeltaRCM) |
| **Wanker et al., April 2026. Field validation of a curved river split.** | Tracking 376 tagged gravel clasts through a Jefferson River junction showed that upstream cross-channel position and distance mattered. The best-performing split rule for the measured northern branch accounted for sediment arriving unevenly across the channel because of upstream curvature. Use this case to evaluate how much upstream geometry a local solve needs. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JF008799) |
| **Nghiem et al., March 2026. Mud supply to Wax Lake islands.** | In field measurements and supporting hydraulic calculations, channel levees sometimes blocked wetland inflow. Tides modulated sediment concentration, upstream river discharge drove much of the sediment flux into the observed island, and settling of aggregated mud contributed to accretion. This makes channel-to-floodplain exchange and mud retention measurable targets. [Field study](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025WR042769) |
| **Wu et al., March 2026. Basin-scale river migration.** | The study coupled hydrology, geomorphology, and tectonics, reconstructed channel movement over 2000–2021 in a study basin, and used a surrogate for parameter uncertainty analysis. Its application here is to compare evolving catchments against mapped channel movement over time. [Paper and assets](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-30-1563-2026) |
| **Barile et al., 2025. Sediment division at real delta junctions.** | Calibrated hydraulics and particle tracking across five Wax Lake bifurcations, with available sediment measurements, show why branch depth and width and cross-channel bathymetry matter. Compare the division of water and each sediment fraction separately. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JF008152) |
| **Monsalve et al., 2025. RiverBedDynamics.** | The Landlab component couples hydraulics to Exner bed mass balance and evolving grain mixtures. It was compared against analytical and previously reported solutions and demonstrated on a synthetic watershed. Use it as an inland gravel transport reference. [Paper and software](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-18-3427-2025) |

Two recent preprints have narrower uses. **Davis, September 2026** gives a cacheable, deterministic river routing scheme for procedural terrain. It conditions elevation along paths already traced within tectonic plates. Borrow ideas for caching only where cross-basin drainage and boundary flux remain consistent. **Li, July 2026 (Inunda)** reports 0.67 m mean absolute error against surveyed high-water marks for a Hurricane Harvey hindcast with a differentiable GPU flood solver. It is a lead for offline roughness and infiltration calibration on fixed terrain. [Plate-local routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00301) · [Inunda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09614)

## Reproducible virtual reference sites

1. **Wax Lake Delta hydraulics and sediment transfer.** Start with the archived Delta-X Delft3D Flexible Mesh configuration, which has bathymetry, model setup, and spring and fall runs calibrated and evaluated with remote and in-situ observations. Use discharge and water-level series at the boundaries. Compare interior stage, velocities, inundation, and split discharge against held-out locations or periods. Use surveyed cross sections and sediment observations for the branch and wetland exchanges. The archived runs are hydraulic reference cases. For long-term bed evolution, pair separately dated shorelines and repeat bathymetry where coverage overlaps, and record the uncertainty and vertical datum of each survey. [Delft3D archive](https://daacweb-prod.ornl.gov/DELTAX/guides/DeltaX_Delft3D_FM_HydroModel.html) · [Channel velocity surveys](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/delta-x-acoustic-doppler-current-profiler-channel-surveys-mrd-louisiana-2021-v2-b91fe) · [NASA shoreline time series](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/world-of-change/wax-lake/)
2. **A gravel river and a contrasting coastal site.** Use the USGS Puyallup channel-change data for an independent inland sediment and migration reference. Use the USGS lower Nooksack river and delta study to check tidal and river boundary interactions under a different coastal setting. Keep flood extent verification separate from erosion and deposition verification. [Puyallup data](https://www.usgs.gov/data/supporting-datasets-analyses-sediment-transport-and-channel-change-puyallup-river-watershed) · [Nooksack study and model](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20235047)
3. **Idealized solver comparisons.** Use lake-at-rest, wetting and drying, and dam-break cases with known solutions. Use the Song and Zhang setups for moving-bed cases with fixed inputs and different end states. Run these at the same elapsed hydraulic and morphological times and record any morphological acceleration. [SWASHES exact solutions](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.3741) · [Delft3D kernels](https://github.com/Deltares/Delft3D)
4. **Large-river branch dynamics.** Use the Middle Yangtze model outputs as a comparison case for bank retreat and for the division of flow and sediment at large inland bifurcations. The authors' archive contains outputs from a model calibrated and validated with field measurements at two reaches. Treat those simulated profiles as model comparisons and reserve measured profiles for independent validation. [Author data archive](https://zenodo.org/records/15278750)

## World-generation design derived from the research

- **Catchment.** Route rainfall, snowmelt, soil storage, and baseflow into a connected network above chunk scale. Deliver seasonal discharge hydrographs and sediment loads by class to the lower reaches, with tributaries entering at their actual confluences. Recalculate drainage after meaningful changes to the bed. The offline catchment review already exercises rerouting and paired budgets on a changing surface. Its 4 m and 2 m comparisons also show why visual shape and numerical convergence need separate checks. [Local catchment review](CATCHMENT_REVIEW.md)
- **Lower river and sea.** Evolve a continuous domain extending from upstream of the backwater and floodplain transition through the shelf. Begin with conservative, well-balanced 2D shallow-water fluxes and positivity-preserving wetting and drying. Advance the bed through a conservative sediment balance with an active surface layer, separate bedload and suspended load, settling and entrainment, and erodible banks. Add tide and wave shear where indicated by the coast. Use the reference sites to decide whether vertical stratification or cohesive flocculation needs an explicit model or a calibrated approximation. Distributary openings, closure, levee overtopping, and deposits can alter the next flow step across the whole reach.
- **Voxel and multiplayer.** Start with nested 8, 4, and 2 m hydraulic comparisons and refine where cross sections, bars, or conveyance change materially. Preserve fine-scale storage and connected channel capacity across coarse cells. Check features on 1, 0.5, and 0.25 m voxel outputs with the same physical inputs and water datum. Bake coupled evolution above chunk scale and version the resulting terrain and water fields. For authoritative multiplayer, make the server's bake or immutable cached tiles the source of truth. Use fixed ordering and stable seed-derived event sequences. Verify cross-platform hashes before relying on independent floating-point recomputation. Keep player water simulation and rendering on their existing separate budgets.

## Acceptance evidence

| Layer | What to compare on held-out cases |
| --- | --- |
| Water | Stage and discharge hydrographs, flood peak time, inundation overlap, wet/dry arrival, cross-section velocity, and per-branch discharge fraction. Report timing, magnitude, and observation error alongside the water budget. |
| Sediment and terrain | Sediment flux by grain class, bed elevation change, sediment retained and exported, lateral migration, and the age and location of new bars. Repeat with a smaller cell size and time step and a lower morphology acceleration factor. |
| Delta form | Width and sinuosity along the lower river, distributary connectivity, channel and island area distributions, elevation above a common datum, and evolution over successive dates. Distinguish intertidal bars from persistent islands and keep coastal regimes separate when comparing distributions. |
| Game | Same-seed cross-platform hashes for authoritative data, continuity across adjacent tiles and all three voxel scales, bake time and peak memory, native screenshots and traversal at matched views, then an actual two-client run. |

Preview 224 is a comparison point. It bakes the lower reach and shelf together with a mobile bed and no authored distributary fan. The three surveyed seeds (16, 74, 731) had water paths from the inlet to the sea. At a 2 m survey spacing, none produced detached islands meeting the review's size band of 12–1200 m² and height cutoff of 1 m above sea level. Two runs of seed 74 on one machine matched the physical and per-voxel-size hashes. These checks cover connectivity and a limited repeatability case. Field accuracy, native appearance, multiplayer, and performance budgets for this preview remain unverified.

The first calibration milestone is a Wax Lake fixed-bed run with held-out water levels and branch discharges, followed by one independently assessed mobile-bed interval. Compare the full reference and AXIOM's reduced bake using the same geometry, forcings, and elapsed time. Keep the reference outputs beside each simplification so any loss of channel or island behavior can be traced to a particular change.

## Wax Lake junction replay and preview 232

`cargo run --locked --offline -p engine_worldgen --example wax_lake_replay` compares two observed tidal stages at the junction of Main and Gadwall Passes. It supplies surveyed width and area plus the measured upstream discharge. Both branch discharges are held out and no roughness parameters are fitted. On June 18, the upstream discharge was interpolated to the midpoint of the two branch surveys. On June 19, the available upstream survey preceded both branch surveys. The measured branch sums differed from the imposed totals by 18.3 and 23.0 m³/s, respectively. These are sequential field observations, so the mismatch contributes to absolute error. [Source measurements, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014WR016149)

| Field run | Measured Main share | Width-only error | Conveyance error | Conveyance MAE, both branches |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Falling tide, June 18, 2014 | 26.23% | 19.89 pp | 0.34 pp | 9.13 m³/s |
| Rising tide, June 19, 2014 | 26.45% | 20.19 pp | 0.70 pp | 12.18 m³/s |

The replay uses relative wide-channel Manning conveyance, `A × (A/W)^(2/3)`, assuming a common hydraulic gradient and roughness. It establishes a junction-scale relationship on surveyed geometry. It does not measure hydraulic timing or sediment accretion. The fall 2021 Delft3D mesh and hourly output were located through NASA's catalog. Both official download paths returned HTTP 401 for an unauthenticated download. A full domain comparison requires the Earthdata files and co-timed stage measurements. [Delft3D inputs and outputs](https://doi.org/10.3334/ORNLDAAC/2464) · [Water surface elevation observations](https://doi.org/10.3334/ORNLDAAC/2086)

Planet preview 232 uses the same conveyance relationship to distribute its imposed inflow over the wetted cross-section. Downstream fluxes follow the solved water and terrain. Sand moves along the bed with the local current, and suspended fines can settle in shallow water during slack flow. Both transfers update the bed for the next hydraulic step. The bake still uses the existing bounded tile count and hydraulic grid, then stores a compact field for all three voxel sizes. The generator version travels in the existing multiplayer world identity.

Run a coastal survey with `cargo run --locked --offline -p engine_worldgen --example morphodynamics_review -- 74 232`. Compare with version 231 using the same build, seed, and viewpoint. Save promotion needs native terrain review and an independent mobile-bed comparison.

The preview 232 survey kept all three sampled mouths connected. The local bed crossed from below sea level to more than 0.15 m above it on 1,088 m² in seed 731, 768 m² in seed 16, and 0 m² in seed 74. The 2 m planform survey found two small detached areas in seed 16 at 1 m above the sea datum. They were also present in version 231. No new detached island passed the survey's 12–1,200 m² size gate. The matched terrain maps still show single, relatively simple approaches. Two runs of seed 74 matched the Planet checksum and the physical and voxel hashes for all three voxel sizes on one Windows machine. The 4 m coastal bake took 1.26–5.05 s across these runs on a shared build machine. The measurement excludes native frame time and varies with concurrent load.

### Open field data comparisons

The public 2014 Wax Lake sediment record is downloadable anonymously. Run `python tools/wax_lake_sediment_replay.py <directory-with-the-two-published-CSVs>`. The earliest 1,740 valid observations calibrate the current-only and current-plus-wave stress proxies, and the later 1,740 evaluate them. Held-out mean absolute stress error was 0.01077 Pa using currents and 0.00891 Pa with waves. Bias fell from −0.00760 to −0.00091 Pa. The observed wave height is supplied to the proxy. Of 33 held-out, coincident SSC readings, the six with stress at least 0.02 Pa averaged 201.90 mg/L, compared with 102.68 mg/L below 0.01 Pa (21 readings). This is a local wave and sediment check on one wetland record. The improvement makes an explicit wave stress field a candidate for future solver work. Preview 232 still uses its inherited coastal wave forcing. [Open field CSVs and unit legend](https://doi.org/10.17632/sb4xnz5ncv.2) · [Field study](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JC016146)

`python tools/delta_survey_change.py ew12_aug_dem_1m.asc ew14_sept_dem_1m.asc` compares the co-registered USGS Elwha bed surveys over 1,198,016 m² of common coverage. The observed net bed gain was 2,479,231 m³. Area averaging preserved that signed volume. Separate erosion dropped from 23,294 m³ at 1 m to 22,600 m³ at 4 m and 21,498 m³ at 8 m because erosion and deposition cancel within coarse cells. This surveyed dam-removal interval is an independent wave-influenced example for resolution loss. It supplies observed bed change for a future forced model comparison. [August 2012 survey](https://doi.org/10.5066/F7PN93SB) · [September 2014 survey](https://doi.org/10.5066/F7P55KN0)

In a localhost session at 0.5 m voxel size, the host and two headless clients built seed 74, version 232 with the same executable. Both clients completed the join and moved. This covers the local session and world recipe. Cross-platform, WAN, and rendered multiplayer coverage remain open.
