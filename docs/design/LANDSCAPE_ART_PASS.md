# AXIOM landscape art pass

Give a region a silhouette the player can remember, a route they can read from the ground, and a reason to take it. Keep the chunky pixel clusters, editable voxel forms and limited material ramps from [AXIOM art direction](../../ART_DIRECTION.md) and the [pixel-art guide](PIXEL_ART_DIRECTION.md). Put the strongest contrast where the player should look next.

## What the coastal capture needs

The rebuilt mouth has a readable split from above. From the bank, the similar-height sand ribbons compete with each other, isolated trees feel evenly spaced, and the far ridge closes the water against a grey horizon. A straight color break across the foreground water interrupts the scene. The existing capture predates the revised coastal palette, so it can establish those composition issues and the visible seam but cannot judge the new ramps.

Start the player below a pale bluff, with a dark stand of trees gathered in the sheltered ground. The bluff briefly hides part of the mouth. Then the path rounds its toe and opens onto a low sand crescent, a hooked bar and a gap between distant headlands. Put the one warm accent on a small timber landing or a half-buried quay where a sheltered back channel actually reaches walkable ground. Leave open water around it. A small opening in the trees should mark the route back inland.

| Scale | Art decision | Grounding in the world |
|---|---|---|
| Skyline | One dominant shoulder, one lower countershape, one gap toward the sea | Use actual coastal relief and open water. Favor a headland that can frame a route from more than one approach. |
| Midground | Broad cream sand, jade over the submerged tip, a dark olive wetland, deep teal through the fairway | Put sand on existing bars and shelves. Keep sheltered mud and reed pockets distinct from exposed gravel and rock. |
| Walking height | Dark trunks, a broken line of reeds, a worn step down to water, one ochre or vermilion accent | Put detail at shade boundaries, contact points and the working edge of the landing. Leave the walked bank open. |
| Motion and depth | Small current glints at bends, foam at contact, sky-colored far slopes | Reuse existing water and atmosphere passes. Keep the water seam a separate review item and check the scene in noon light. |

## Composition families

| Family | Dominant form and quiet space | Route and destination | Palette relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cliff estuary | Pale bluff against a wide horizontal channel | Walk the inner crescent to a sheltered landing | Oyster sand against olive woodland, jade and petrol blue |
| Low marsh delta | A single dark island or grove above broad, low reed flats | Follow a raised natural bank to a ferry point or weathered marker | Celadon channels, straw reeds and plum-brown mud |
| Inland meander | Tall shaded forest wall opposite an open terrace | A gravel ford leads to a glade or riverside workshop | Cool canopy masses, ochre gravel and a narrow sunlit green reach |
| Alpine gorge | Two unequal rock shoulders and a long light gap | Traverse a talus bench to a pass, lookout or footbridge | Slate and rust rock, blue-violet shade and chalk snow |
| Badlands basin | One eroded mesa above a broad dry floor | Follow a dry wash into a shaded cleft or abandoned cistern | Apricot strata and pale straw, with dark violet in the cut |
| Volcanic coast | Dark headland and a quiet ash beach | Follow a lava bench to a sheltered inlet or old loading ledge | Charcoal and mineral ochre, cool haze, local heat color at active sources |

Let each family change the crossing, the view and the kind of ground underfoot. Many reaches can be quiet transitions. A regional landmark gains scale from the ordinary ground around it.

## Romantic landscape studies

Friedrich gives the player a place to stand and look. Martin makes the climb, descent or crossing feel immense. Use both at different distances within the same region. A quiet outlook makes an enormous landscape feel more convincing when the player has just walked there through ordinary ground.

| Painting | Playable translation |
|---|---|
| [Caspar David Friedrich, Wanderer above the Sea of Fog](https://cdfriedrich.de/en/chronicle/wanderer-ueber-dem-nebelmeer/) | Climb a wooded saddle onto a dark rock spur. Under low fog, two or three distant ridges emerge like islands, while a lower shoulder hides the river. In clear weather, the same ledge lets the player trace the river down to a crossing. The rock lip and an optional cairn supply first-person scale. |
| [Friedrich, Chalk Cliffs on Rügen](https://digital.kmw.ch/en/art-objects/caspar-david-friedrich-chalk-cliffs-ruegen/) | Frame a blue sea through an asymmetric opening between pale rock shoulders. | [Friedrich, The Monk by the Sea](https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/alte-nationalgalerie/collection-research/conservation-care/caspar-david-friedrich-project/) | Give exposed coasts a quiet stretch of wet sand and long sky. One distant beacon or silhouette is enough to establish scale and direction. Let players leave the crowded part of the world behind for a while. |
| [Friedrich, The Sea of Ice](https://cdfriedrich.de/en/chronicle/das-eismeer/) | Give a glacial basin a few leaning slabs and deep blue gaps, with a broken hull barely legible at their feet. Offer a route around the fractured ice and a short salvage route between the masses. |

The strongest first study is the estuary bluff in two conditions. In clear air, the eye follows the river bend to the jade bar and crossing. In low fog, the near spur, the tips of distant headlands and a pale gap toward the sea carry the view. The next sightline comes from the shore looking back up at the same climb. Use the existing terrain and fog systems, and keep a usable route under both conditions. Save Martin's vast terraced basin and catastrophic landscapes for rarer geological candidates.

Preview generator 195 surveys a dry shoulder near an estuary and checks its view toward open water. A short graded approach and small clearing expose the view while keeping the terrain profile and vegetation outside that footprint. The survey can skip a river mouth. Run `morphodynamics_review 4 195` for the approach and lookout camera positions. The view and route are the first study, with local mist and a landing still separate art decisions.

## Applying the pass

1. Review the two existing estuary seeds at bank level, from the reverse bank and overhead. Establish where the currently approved route, water level, bluff candidate and sightlines actually lie. Sketch the same scene as four value masses before placing assets.
2. Pick one coastal composition whose bluff and open bank already follow the terrain. Reuse solved flow, deposits and exposure for the shape, terrain masks for habitat, and existing woodland clusters and settlement pieces for the walkable approach. Admit a landing only where the dry approach, sheltered berth and sightline agree.
3. Record a before and after at the same seed, physical voxel scale, camera, time and weather. Walk from upstream to the opening at noon and at a second light angle. Review a contrasting seed and the same metre positions at 1 m, 0.5 m and 0.25 m. Check frame timing separately from image capture.

Prefer a seed-derived regional placement evaluated at generation time, shared by resident and distant terrain. Preserve the solved water surface, existing edit ownership and old save behavior through the existing generator versioning where geometry changes. Compare map-to-ground silhouette and route continuity before adding foliage or new materials. Keep bounded placement, existing render passes and the ordinary streaming budget.
