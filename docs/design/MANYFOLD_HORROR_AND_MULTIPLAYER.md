# Manyfold horror and multiplayer design

Date: 2026-10-29. Lore direction and implementation requirements; encounters below are not shipped features.

Extend the Manyfold as a place players can inhabit, investigate, fear, and change. Its streets belong to the history of the Anchor. Its impossible connections obey discoverable rules. Expeditions should leave behind opened roads, rescued residents, damaged machinery, and useful discoveries that survive the session.

Use this brief for the horror and spatial content in the supplied Manyfold and Webway prompts. Keep the existing engine, progression, art direction, controls, and multiplayer authority. Implement bounded expeditions through the current milestone process in [decision 0039](../decisions/0039-axiom-expansion-briefs.md). This brief does not replace the engine roadmap or authorize a new networking stack.

## Place in the existing world

**Design facts.** The Manyfold contains districts removed from endangered places and connected through evacuation infrastructure. Later occupants repaired and expanded that infrastructure. Some custodians still prevent departures under orders intended to protect evacuees. Local rooms, buildings, and player alterations retain their identities when a connection changes.

**Records.** Anchor manifests list freight sent to receiving halls absent from ordinary maps. Ballast households reused those halls, built kitchens around their pipes, and marked dependable routes. These records connect the Manyfold to the Anchor without identifying its original builders. Claims that it saved whole worlds during repeated catastrophes belong in incomplete evacuation records, not an omniscient history screen.

**Rumours.** Some residents call the lower districts Hell. Others say the missing ninth office admitted their ancestors. Neither claim resolves Return, the Surveyors, the scraped name, or the people above the braid. The Manyfold is not the answer to every existing mystery.

The Crimson Nexus retains its separate multiversal role. Manyfold travel opens particular discovered connections with explicit access conditions. A door is not a universal destination menu. Existing Nether or hell environments retain their own geography; the inhabitants' religious names do not silently identify them with this dimension.

Keep the seven existing regions: Foldfields, Bellworks, Cistern of Borrowed Rain, House of Contrary Stairs, Unfinished Borough, Guest Wing, and Room with an Outside. The new encounters occupy these regions. Preserve Stillcourt and the Long Guest. The final measuring guardian is a custodian; calling it the “Last Surveyor” must not establish that it belongs to the unexplained Surveyor civilization.

## Descent and Purgatorio

The descent passes through consequences of the evacuation system: waiting halls that never discharge their occupants, stores withheld from hungry streets, detention chambers treated as accommodation, and homes reconstructed from incomplete memories. Each district has living residents with their own explanation of what happened. A lower floor need not be older: displaced districts keep their original era, visible in their materials and repairs.

Use Dante's progression through an underworld and an ascent toward release as a structural influence. Purgatorio is the inhabited route back toward the Room with an Outside. Stillcourt is its first dependable refuge; repaired landings, gardens, workshops, and guest rooms form the ascent. Its progress is practical: clear a blocked evacuation route, restore clean water, release a captive, or make a dangerous crossing usable. A repair benefits later travellers. Residents may disagree about whether this amounts to redemption. [Dante reference](https://danteworlds.laits.utexas.edu/purgatory/index.html).

Do not make nine circles into nine equipment tiers, equate them with the ninth office, or assign players a hidden moral score. The routes can cross and reconnect. Players may retreat early, build a home, return with help, or continue below. The Room with an Outside remains the finite destination of the main expedition; unexplored frontier branches can continue afterward.

Mental descent comes through isolation, fixation, grief, doubt, and the temptation to accept a comforting explanation. Show these in people and places. Avoid diagnoses as enemy classes, a universal sanity meter, or random loss of input. A frightened character can remain competent. Rest, company, repair, and choosing to leave must have room in the experience.

## Four encounters to develop

### The sealed house

An Anchor pressure station became a Ballast household. It now occupies an annex of the House of Contrary Stairs. A pulse travels through its floor; newer mortar seals a room excluded from the household plan. One surviving account calls the room a burial, another a quarantine.

Players can compare the pulse with the pump cycle, inspect the mortar, interview a resident when dialogue supports it, or open a service passage. Cutting power quiets the machinery and disables a lift; it need not silence whatever is inside. Bracing, drainage, excavation, and an alternate supply are valid approaches. The chamber's contents are fixed when the encounter is created. Observation must not reroll the answer.

Opening the chamber releases its actual occupant or contents into the shared world. Keep the initial consequence local and recoverable. A repaired station supplies the household and opens a return stair. Players who break straight through the wall still reach a real chamber and face the resulting pressure or structural problem.

Lore slot: Anchor era, pressure control, patched pipe and conflicting household plans, Sluice and held breath, why the room was sealed.

### The familiar street

In the Unfinished Borough, a reception system offers arriving travellers a familiar face. It uses authored memories of people the character has met in the game. One player may see a missing guide; another sees the same physical figure as an old neighbour. Its position, movement, touch, and damage remain shared.

The figure repeats a remembered conversation but cannot account for something that happened afterward. Its imitation has a bounded repertoire. Players can compare accounts, ask an available question, inspect the receiver feeding it, or follow it while a companion watches the exit. Voice chat is allowed to solve the discrepancy; it cannot defeat the underlying spatial or physical problem by itself.

An imitation can be a lure, a confused surviving person, or a shelter's attempted kindness. Establish the encounter's actual behaviour beforehand. Do not reveal that every friendly resident is a monster. The larger uncertainty is what the reception system preserved and what it attracted.

Never read personal files, infer real bereavements, clone a player's voice, or impersonate actual chat or server messages. The source is authored game history. A reduced-distortion presentation keeps the evidence and objective usable.

Lore slot: post-evacuation reception retrofit, welcoming displaced residents, duplicate arrival portraits, the Ballast's household records, whose memory survives in the apparatus.

### The repeated landing

In the Bellworks, three similar landings carry different repairs. A working bell changes one marked connection. A second fixed stair returns to the refuge. The objective is to deliver a brass weight to a real counterweight socket, opening a useful freight route.

Players can follow the warm pipe, distinguish mortar repairs, observe the bell sequence, or watch from opposite ends. A chalk mark stays on the same landing. Connecting a new ordinary passage by mining is valid where the rooms are locally adjacent. A remote landing requires its fold, even if the player removes the wall around the doorway.

A solo player can operate a latch to hold a useful state. Additional players can observe, haul, brace, or defend. Do not require exactly two bodies or simultaneous switches unless an accessible solo mechanism provides the same operation.

Lore slot: Anchor switching machinery with Ballast repairs, freight routing, counterweight plates, the Watch timetable, a destination absent from the surviving schedule.

### The unfolding instrument

In a later archive, an object small enough for a cabinet unfolds into a room-sized arrangement of luminous surfaces. Each stage exposes a limited set of traversable or visible cross-sections. Apertures carry explicit rules: view only, traversal, or material transfer. Players see and manipulate the same stage.

The instrument can reveal a missing room or a survey route. Advancing it requires power and changes a known connection. Dust, dropped weights, and light establish what passes through each aperture before a dangerous use. The far side can contain evidence of another observer without a final explanation of its origin.

Implement the first version as authored stages and ordinary local spaces. It represents an encounter with higher dimensions; it is not proof that the engine simulates four-dimensional matter.

Lore slot: Anchor instrument assembled around an older geometric fragment, inspection, etched planes and a cabinet too small for them, survey marks, who can see through the reverse side.

## Architecture and pacing

Place each anachronism where its history or function can be investigated: a lit queue display inside a stone chapel, a clean bath beneath a collapsed school, a telephone connected to a sealed receiving booth, an electric clock fitted over an older bell dial. Pick one conspicuous mismatch per scene and support it with wires, water, wear, or repairs. Avoid random household props scattered as “dream logic.”

Maintain the established pixel materials and authored colour. Use warm inhabited courtyards, pale exterior mazes, wet service corridors, and dark interiors in contrast. Constant fog, monochrome grading, distorted text, and relentless noises would flatten the places into one mood.

Alternate travel, observation, threat, and relief. Repeated spaces need identifiable differences. Threats have quiet periods and readable approaches. The Long Guest remains a creature with a habitat and possible release route; it does not become a random chase trigger. A refuge's protection is a dependable rule, even when characters question its history.

## Spatial rules

Distinguish four capabilities in code, documentation, and review:

1. **Folded topology:** persistent local regions joined by explicit portal edges. A stair can return beneath its entrance while every room uses ordinary local coordinates.
2. **Variable gravity:** a field changes local down. The existing radial tube belongs here. Gravity alone does not make its metric hyperbolic.
3. **Optical or geometric deformation:** declare whether only a view changes or collision and material geometry also change. A visual effect cannot silently stand in for physical space.
4. **Curved metric space:** a later experiment requiring consistent distance, movement, collision, rays, and material rules. Hyperbolic geometry and anti-de Sitter spacetime are distinct. AdS is a spacetime model, not a synonym for an impossible corridor. An AdS-inspired visual environment must be labelled as such unless its spacetime dynamics are actually implemented. [Physics reference](https://preposterousuniverse.com/wp-content/uploads/grnotes-eight.pdf).

Start portal traversal with rigid transforms at unit scale and preserved local up. Extend rotations only through supported body, camera, projectile, audio, and collision paths. Keep the [Reality Contract](../NEW/AXIOM_Reality_Contract_v1.txt) distinction between optical paths, bodies, gravity, and material transfer.

An apparently endless fall loops through a finite, identified shaft. A ledge, side exit, rope attachment, or control provides escape. A fall-loop recovery cache belongs to a stable alcove. Neither a player's body nor their inventory is copied on repeated crossings.

## Generate puzzles before corridors

Build a finite expedition graph with an entrance, refuge, objective, return path, alternate approach, and optional branch. Assign authored puzzle templates to compatible rooms. Generate geometry around that graph, then validate the actual voxel clearances and interaction points.

Each template specifies its initial state, controllable parts, objective predicate, clue placements, required resources, alternative methods, persistent consequences, and recovery route. The predicate concerns the world: a weight reaches a socket, water leaves a shaft, a captive crosses a threshold. Reading a note does not set an invisible permission flag.

Validate required resources as well as connectivity. A key cannot require its own locked route. A generator cannot require fuel available only beyond the door it powers. Count access from each participant's possible side of a changed connection. Bounded generation attempts fall back to a known valid layout.

Continue frontier generation from stable region IDs and a pinned ruleset. Set budgets for branching, active regions, resident memory, and portal views. Persist discoveries and edits before unloading. Do not regenerate visited streets to manufacture disorientation or make the host process an infinite graph.

## Shared world and differing perceptions

The host owns topology, collision, gravity rules, creatures, puzzle state, damage, items, and recovery. Verified integer matter domains keep the existing accepted-command replication; other domains use the established authoritative state path. Relays and rendering clients do not decide outcomes.

An authored perceptual effect may change a face, a line spoken by an apparition, or a local sound. It cannot privately change support beneath a player's feet, the destination of a traversable door, or whether an attack hits. A physical illusion uses the same underlying collision for everyone. Store who received a clue when that history affects later events; do not derive clues from frame rate or wall-clock randomness.

Use stable identities for regions, edges, encounters, objects, and actors. Conceptual records, to be adapted to the existing save and wire structures:

- Region: seed, generator/ruleset version, persistent edit revision, gravity profile, refuge and recovery references.
- Connection: endpoints, rigid transform, topology revision, current destination, warning and commit ticks, anchor and permission state.
- Encounter: template/version, phase, physical object IDs, spent resources, completion and reward transaction IDs.
- Perception: encounter ID, recipient actor ID, authored variant, start/end tick, clue history.

Connection changes close new admission, finish or cancel outstanding crossings and transfers, check destination readiness, commit once, then reopen. Clients acknowledge the usable destination state; an unready client waits behind a clear barrier. A stale command is rejected or retried against the new revision. Preserve an established exit for players already inside.

Entity transfer has one commit and one physical owner. Transform position, orientation, and velocity consistently. Do not interpolate a remote player through the stone between unrelated regions. Drops, projectiles, AI sensing, and audio need bounded traversal rules of their own. Initially exclude unsupported fluid, rope, and power connections across portals with visible boundaries; extend them only with explicit transfer accounting.

One player operating a gate cannot duplicate a weight another player is carrying. Rewards and item transfers use idempotent operations. Late joiners receive the current encounter and topology revisions before interacting. Reload and reconnect preserve repairs, opened routes, occupants, reward claims, and recovery locations. A disconnected participant cannot hold a cooperative mechanism forever.

Pursuers such as the Second Self use a bounded record of actual movement and observed actions. They cannot read future inputs, teleport into an unrelated home, copy inventories, ignore claims, or reset themselves by forcing a reconnect. The Openhand Key and gate controls respect server ownership permissions; lore powers do not grant administrator privileges. Keep existing PvP and death settings.

## First implementation and acceptance

Current source anchors, inspected 2026-10-29:

| Owner | Present source and required extension |

| `crates/app/src/world/storage.rs` | Weight state is saved. Region, connection, and encounter records still need their own versioned persistence. |
| `crates/netcode/src/pose.rs` | Packed pose contains chunk position and yaw/pitch. Audit both this representation and the application pose before adding region identity or orientation semantics. |

The first new expedition is the repeated landing: one refuge, three identifiable landings, one supported fold, one switchable connection, one shared brass weight, one lasting shortcut. Keep it dry and use ordinary up. The sealed house and familiar street follow once shared traversal and persistence work. The unfolding instrument, metric curvature, and a dragon body spanning portals are later work.

Require a plan row and explicit save/protocol compatibility handling before the first runtime slice. Pin existing worlds to their generator; do not replace their terrain. Keep generation rules and budgets versioned. Do not add dependencies or restructure the workspace to mirror the supplied prompt's hypothetical crate tree.

Acceptance requires a solo expedition and two connected players, with evidence of:

- A carried weight and both actors crossing a fold once, with matching destination and orientation.
- A gate changing while players stand on opposite sides, retaining an exit and rejecting stale interactions.
- Digging or construction providing a valid alternate solution.
- One reward when players complete the same objective together.
- Join, disconnect, save, and reload preserving the route, marks, weight, repairs, and reward claim.
- Delayed destination loading preventing unsafe traversal; bounded portal and simulation work.
- Later perceptual variants producing different accounts while the physical encounter stays shared.

These are acceptance requirements, not results. Add or run tests only when requested under the workspace instructions. Native play evidence is required before claiming the expedition's feel, visual quality, or multiplayer readiness.

## Player text

Use [WORLD_LORE.md](WORLD_LORE.md): a place, an object, a useful fact, and occasionally one troubling detail. Keep literary references in scene structure, recurring objects, and conflicting testimony. Familiarity with an author must never be required to solve a puzzle.

Candidate records, to place only alongside the corresponding working encounter:

> Sluice note: “Pump isolated. The pulse continued.”

> Ballast repair: “Leave this stair. It still reaches the kitchen.”

> Receiving book: “Same passenger. Different next of kin.”

> Mortar tally: “Bricks counted from outside. Tools counted from within.”

Use direct interaction labels: **Hold gate**, **Place weight**, **Brace wall**, **Return to courtyard**. Describe costs and hazards plainly. Cut generic promises of mystery, repeated grand titles, invented psychological gauges, and narration that tells players what to feel.
