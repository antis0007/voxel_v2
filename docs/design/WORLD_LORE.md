# World lore: the Anchor and the Braid

Date: 2026-10-22. Current. Read this before writing any player-facing text: inscriptions,
prop names, `Inspect` lines, quest routes, boss titles, journal entries.

Reading order: the one fact, **The ninth office**, **How to extend this world**, **Deep time**, then **After the fall** and **The living powers** for the inhabited world. Everything else is reference.

## The one fact everything hangs from

The hills are the roof of a city that lowered itself into the ground on purpose.

The city was the ground anchor of the Meridian Lift. The tether (the *braid*) needs
weight at the bottom. Counterweights, patient bells, chain lifts, ballast and freight
cradles in the Undercrown are not decoration: they are the anchor's machinery. The
surface ruins (workshop, watchtower, waterworks, foundry, conservatory, archive,
toll crossing) were the anchor's *offices*, poking up through the soil.

When the last ascent left, most people went up the braid to the crowns and the orbital
foundries. Some stayed below, because an anchor that empties is not an anchor. They
called themselves **the Ballast**. The Undercrown's first inscription is their creed:
*"For those who carry the burden, the road descends."*

The player never reads that summary anywhere. They put it together.

## The economy: the tithe of sparks

The anchor ran on sparks given by hand, one at a time, and every gift was recorded.
Makers signed each coil with a tally (sparks given, sparks owed). Travellers going up
left a spark at the toll for those staying down. The Archive's final ledger lists the
Ballast by name.

When the people were gone, the machines kept the books. **Guardians are office
wardens still collecting a tithe that nobody owes.** They are not evil and they are not
alive in any way that matters. Each one is doing its job correctly against records that
stopped being true. This is why the Last Tollkeeper fights at a gate its own keeper left
open.

The player's first act, restoring the workshop heart, is the first willing spark given
in a very long time. That is why the ember hollow's coal starts beating again.

## The braid still runs

Meridian carriages descend every three world days (see `MERIDIAN_ORBITAL_SERVICE.md`),
hold, and climb. They carry correspondence and a "narrow service return". Nobody knows
who it is addressed to. The watchtower log recorded every descent until the entries
stop. The carriages did not stop.

What is up there, whether anyone is, and why the service still pays a return to an
empty anchor are open questions. **Keep them open.** Hint; never resolve in one line.

## Threads to pull (do not close them all)

- **The ninth office.** Every directory has eight offices and a ninth line painted over. See **The ninth office** below. This is the main secret.
- **The scraped name.** One line of the Ballast ledger was erased by someone who knew it.
  The Unwritten Keeper guards it. Candidate answers: the last keeper; someone who went up
  after promising to stay; the player's own line. Never confirm.
- **"Paid. Go up."** Someone settled their tally and left. The tollkeeper's timber says
  nothing is owed. The wardens disagree.
- **The flawed coupling.** The founders shipped a known flaw upward. Strands failed later
  (the choir). Cause and effect are on two different walls in two different sites.
- **The held breath.** A water-weight unit at the waterworks, a literal breath tied in a
  knot in the drowned grotto. The Undercrown's air bells and breath timer make it bodily.
- **The return.** The braid keeps delivering. Something above still thinks the anchor
  is inhabited, or knows it is again.
- **Undercrown leads** (spec: `superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-undercrown-region-design.md`):
  the waterfall door, the singing ice (the choir's lower register), the vanished barge,
  the missing partner in the bridge village. Each should touch one thread above.

## Voice

Three influences, one rule each:

- **Zork:** place first, terse, concrete. Second person only in prompts ("You commit the
  detail to memory"). Name objects plainly so they can be looked for: *blue lamp*, *chain
  lift*, *waterfall door, still shut*. Wit is dry and rare.
- **Neal Stephenson:** the mystery lives in systems and paperwork. Ledgers, gauges, order
  books, tolerances, units of measure, timetables. A number or a procedure is more
  unsettling than an adjective. Infrastructure has opinions; engineers have handwriting.
- **RimWorld:** the small human detail that implies a life. A note in a second hand, a
  meal kept warm, a banked fire, a scraped line. One per text, not three.

Rules:

- An inscription is two or three sentences, under about 220 characters. Fact, detail,
  turn. End on the turn.
- Every new text references at least one shared motif (sparks/tallies, the braid, the
  Ballast, ballast and weight, the ledger, the timetable) and at most one other office.
  That cross-link is what makes the world feel like one place.
- Never explain the Anchor outright. No "long ago, the ancients…". No capitalised
  Prophecy. No villain monologue. The Undercrown has no dialogue system; a person prop's
  line on the prompt card is the whole conversation.
- Benefits (mechanics) stay plain and separate from the story line.
- Avoid the slop words listed under Identity below.
  Prefer: weight, tally, owed, given, braid, held, banked, kept.

## Skyhook and magitech: why the ledger was real

Magitech is the Anchor's word for engineering it stopped explaining. It never skips
physics. A *spark* is charge; a *tally* is a bit; "willingly given" is a clean signal.
Wands are circuits you hold. Resonant crystal is doped substrate. Essence is what the
Ballast called anything they could make but no longer purify.

The braid today is an atmospheric elevator (see `MERIDIAN_ORBITAL_SERVICE.md`). The
future form is a **rotating skyhook**: a momentum-exchange tether that catches payloads
from a suborbital hop and flings them up. Every kilogram lifted costs the hook orbital
momentum; every kilogram lowered pays it back. That is the true ledger. The city lowered
itself, and the Ballast sent ore and water down the braid's return leg, to keep the hook
from decaying. *"For those who carry the burden, the road descends"* is a momentum
budget written as a creed. The "narrow service return" is the hook still paying out
what descending mass earns, to an anchor nobody tends. When the player starts sending
mass down, the return widens.

Design consequence: skyhook throughput is gated by mass the player returns, not by a
currency. Ore, water and spent product are momentum.

## Future focus: semiconductor tiers

The factory's long arc is relearning chipmaking, one historical node at a time. Each
tier is gated by the four real constraints: **purity** (nines of silicon, water, gas),
**feature size** (set by the light you can print with), **cleanliness** (particles per
volume) and **power**. Magitech may rename a step. It may not remove one.

| Tier | Real era | Node | What the player makes | Gate | Office it reopens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Salvage | (the Anchor) | unknown, very small | nothing: *ancient circuits* are scavenged or traded down the braid (ML-C01) | finite supply | expedition sites |
| 1 Crystal detector | 1900s–1940s | point contact | cat's-whisker diode from resonant crystal and copper wire; detector radio, the receiver dish | one crystal, a fine wire | Watch |
| 2 Discrete transistor | 1947–1960 | germanium, then silicon junction | refined silicon from quartz: zone refining, a crystal puller | 9N silicon; a hot, steady furnace | Founders |
| 3 Planar IC | 1960–1975 | 10 µm | contact-mask photolithography; the first integrated "circuits" recipe matured | ultrapure water; UV lamp; filtered air | Sluice, Watch |
| 4 LSI / VLSI | 1975–1995 | 3 µm → 0.35 µm | projection steppers, mercury g/i-line, then KrF 248 nm | cleanroom; reticles; stable power | Artificers |
| 5 Deep submicron | 1995–2006 | 180 → 65 nm | ArF 193 nm, copper interconnect, chemical-mechanical polish | copper purity; slurry; process control | Founders, Archivists (recipes as ledgers) |
| 6 Immersion and FinFET | 2007–2016 | 45 → 14 nm | water-immersion lithography, multipatterning, 3D fins | water again, now optical-grade | Sluice |
| 7 EUV | 2017–2023 | 7 → 3 nm | 13.5 nm light from tin droplets struck by a laser; multilayer mirrors | tin, vacuum, mirror figure; huge power | Choir (mirrors as crystal) |
| 8 Orbital node | beyond | 2 nm, gate-all-around, backside power, and past it | wafers printed only in the Loom's vacuum and microgravity, ridden down the skyhook | skyhook throughput, i.e. returned mass | the crowns |

Notes for whoever builds it:

- The jump between tiers is a *change of light* or a *change of purity*, not a bigger
  number on the same machine. Make each one a new process the player has to set up.
- Tier 0 → 1 is the emotional hinge: the first circuit made instead of found. It should
  feel like the first willing spark (the workshop heart).
- Existing items already sit on the ladder: salvaged and ancient circuits are tier 0;
  `Circuits: 2 copper + 1 tin + 1 crystal` (`factory/recipes.rs`) is a tier 3 planar
  part with magitech names. Rename later; do not rebalance silently.
- Tin is the EUV light source. Water is the immersion lens. Quartz is the wafer. Let
  common surface materials matter again at the top of the tree.
- The scraped name in the ledger and the flawed coupling both belong to tier 8's story:
  the braid is wearing out, and the people above lost the ability to make the parts
  that the people below are now relearning.
- New machines or resources need a save version bump. Prefer modes on existing machines.

## Identity: the maintenance register

This game sounds like **the paperwork of people who kept a large machine running**. The
narrator is a clerk, a field engineer, a quartermaster: precise, a little tired, and
sometimes funny without meaning to be. That is the identity. When text drifts into a
fantasy voice ("ancient power stirs"), it is wrong.

Inspirations, and the one thing taken from each:

| Source | What we take |
| --- | --- |
| RimWorld | object descriptions: what it is, how it is made, what it is for, one dry consequence |
| Noita | magic is a material system; wands are programs; every element interacts |
| FTL | event text is two lines and a decision; consequences are logged, not dramatised |
| Hyper Light Drifter | the ruin tells the story; text is scarce, so each line carries weight |
| Destiny (grimoire) | lore lives in collectible fragments that disagree with each other a little |
| Ringworld | engineering at a scale its inheritors no longer understand; a fall caused by a material failure, not a war |
| Neal Stephenson | systems, units, tolerances, procedures, and the people who maintain them |

### How to write an object description

Third person, present tense. Three parts, in order, one sentence each:

1. **What it is**, in plain words: material, size or origin.
2. **What it is for**, or how it was used.
3. **One consequence or detail** that implies the world: a hazard, a habit, a joke the
   makers would have made.

The RimWorld register:

> *Ancient circuits.* Etched boards salvaged from the anchor's offices, at a feature size no
> living founder can reproduce. Every working machine in the valley started with one.
> Most still carry their maker's tally mark.

The same item as slop:

> *Ancient circuits.* Whispering relics of a forgotten age, humming with mysterious power
> from those who came before.

### Tells that mark text as slop: cut them

- Personifying objects in every line ("has opinions", "decides to", "is hungry"). One
  object in a set can have a personality. Not all of them.
- The words: ancient power, whisper, echo of, testament, forgotten, mysterious, arcane
  energies, those who came before, eldritch, tapestry, realm.
- Three adjectives in a row. "It is not X. It is Y." more than once per screen.
- Explaining the mystery. Ending on a moral.
- Exclamation marks outside a hazard warning.

What good text has instead: a number with a unit, a named procedure, a second hand
writing, a mistake someone made and left in place.

## Magic: what the Anchor called its tools

**Mana** is stored sparks. The wand keeps a tally of what you spend, the same way makers
signed coils. **Blood price** and **Reckless** are the oldest clause in the tithe: when the
sparks run out, the body pays. The Ballast used it in emergencies and wrote down every
time they did.

**Wands** are programming rods from the anchor's work crews. A spell is a routine etched on
a crystal card. Modifiers (`If foe`, `On timer`, `Fork`, `Divide by 2`) are logic. A wand
is a short program read left to right, which is why it behaves like Noita. Most "attack"
spells began as civil tools:

| Spell | Original job |
| --- | --- |
| Lumen drill, Heavy bore | survey and tunnelling; Heavy bore dug the Undercrown |
| Grapple | cable rigging on the chain lifts |
| Rift well, Black hole, Gravity | ballast trimming: sinking or adding mass |
| Timberfall, Meteor | freight-drop and deorbit requests the braid still honours |
| Gale | spore clearance in sealed halls |
| Ice wall, Cold snap | sluice breach seals, timed in held breaths |
| Static charge, Spark | the watch's signal lightning; the tithe unit |
| Touch of gold | an assay routine that reports in gold, for a joke nobody remembers |
| Echo step, Teleport bolt | carriage handoff, scaled down to one body |
| Spore bloom, Mend | licensed conservatory strains for wound care |

**Rare magic** is routines the Ballast never licensed: cards made without a tally mark.
Treat them as contraband, banned for good reasons.

**Elements are materials.** Water conducts, frost shatters, spores burn, oil spreads fire.
New spells extend the material rules. They do not add a new kind of "energy".

## The Bloom: how the Anchor failed

Ringworld's cities fell when a mould ate their superconductor. Ours fell the same way.

The conservatory engineered a fungus to recover dopant metals from spent boards: a
recycling strain. It escaped. It eats the thin metal layers in circuits and leaves the
silicon grey and dead. The Ballast called it **the Bloom**.

What it explains:

- Circuits are salvaged, not made: the Bloom got into the clean rooms first.
- Spore creatures (Spore Sentinel, the Orchard Matron) guard ruins because they are the
  conservatory's work strains, still recycling.
- The chip ladder's **cleanliness** gate is the hard one: a fab is a room the Bloom cannot
  enter. The cleanroom is a quarantine.
- The lanterncap and Mend still work: licensed strains, bred to leave metal alone.
- The keepers went up because the crowns are above the spore line.

Keep the name rare. Show it through grey boards, notes about air filters, and a sealed
door with tape around the frame.

## How to extend this world

This document is a frame, not a story. It fixes a few hard facts and leaves the rest as
labelled gaps. Anyone adding content fills a gap, not a blank page.

### Three levels of truth

Every lore statement belongs to one level. Mark it in design notes; never in game text.

| Level | Meaning | Who may change it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fact** | true in the simulation: the braid exists, the Bloom eats metal, depth is age | only by agreement, and this doc changes first |
| **Record** | what a people wrote down: ledgers, plates, inscriptions, manifests | anyone; records may be wrong, biased or forged |
| **Rumour** | what someone says now: person props, journal hints | anyone; rumours are allowed to contradict each other |

Contradictions between Records are content, not bugs (Destiny's grimoire, Canticle for
Leibowitz). Contradicting a Fact is a bug.

### The lore slot: fill all five for anything new

1. **Era**: which stratum made it (table below).
2. **Purpose**: the job it did. Everything in the world had a job.
3. **Evidence**: the physical thing the player finds. No lore without an object.
4. **Link**: one motif or one other site it points at.
5. **Gap**: the question it raises and does not answer.

A site, item, creature or room that cannot fill all five is not ready.

## Deep time: depth is age

The deeper you dig, the older the builders. This matches the ruin descent layers already
in the generator (fungal, rime, basalt) and the three sanctum landings.

| Stratum | Builders | Layer | What survives | Their unanswered question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Now | the valley: you, and scattered settlers | surface | camps, roads, your workshop | what to rebuild first |
| Above | **the Lifted**: those who rode the braid up | orbit | carriages, crowns, the Loom, the Continuum drums | are they still there, and do they know about us |
| I | **the Ballast**: those who stayed after the Bloom | fungal | kitchens, stills, banked fires, mycelial warrens, catacombs | who scraped the name |
| II | **the Anchor**: the city of offices; the chipmaking peak | rime | offices, sluices, fabs, the Undercrown's machinery, rime geode vaults | why ship the flawed coupling |
| III | **the Surveyors**: whoever placed the braid in the first place | basalt | geometry only: perfectly flat floors, survey marks, the Hall of the Hollow Floor | everything |

Rules:

- The Surveyors are never seen, named by themselves, or explained. They are the
  Ringworld engineers, the monolith. Their work is too large, too exact and too old.
  The Anchor found their survey marks and built on them, as later peoples build on yours.
- Each stratum misunderstood the one below. The Ballast treated Anchor machines as
  shrines. The Anchor treated Surveyor geometry as natural law. The valley treats
  carriages as weather. Write each people's Records with that misunderstanding.
- Between strata there may be gaps nobody can account for. Leave room: a stratum
  "II.5" can be added later without breaking anything.

## Civilisations

Kept vague on purpose. Each entry gives a temperament, a material, a settlement grammar,
and open slots.

**The Surveyors (III).** Material: basalt, fused flat. Grammar: lines, not buildings: a
floor that runs level for a kilometre, a shaft that is exactly vertical, a mark repeated
at a fixed interval. No doors, no writing, no scale for a human body. Open slots: their
units of length; whether the braid was their only work; whether the Hall of Wandering
Stars is theirs or an Anchor copy.

**The Anchor (II).** Material: steel, glass, resonant crystal, silicon. Temperament:
bureaucratic, proud, careful, and at the end, frightened. Grammar: offices around
a machine; every building has a ledger room and a maintenance stair; plaques give
tolerances. Cities were vertical, sunk around the braid's footing, with public
counterweights in the squares. Society: the tithe of sparks; the offices; makers sign
their work. Open slots: their government (council of offices? a single anchor-master?),
their religion if any, their wars if any, the name of the city.

**The Ballast (I).** Material: whatever was left, plus fungus. Temperament: stubborn,
communal, darkly funny. Grammar: households inside Anchor machinery: a kitchen in a
pump hall, a bedroom in a freight cradle, a chapel growing through a prison. They wrote on
Anchor walls, often correcting them. Society: they kept the ledger of who stayed, ran
unregistered stills, bred lanterncaps. Open slots: how long they lasted; what ended them;
whether any are left in the deep sea.

**The Lifted (Above).** Never met. Known from manifests, contract terms (ML-C01),
timetables and the cargo itself. Temperament inferred from paperwork: polite, exact,
and careful never to say who they are. Open slots: all of it. Some Records say they
left in shame, some in triumph; both can be true.

**The valley (Now).** Settlers, traders, cargo-watchers. Factions are open slots. Two
starting suggestions:
- *The Copyists*: keep Anchor ledgers by copying them by hand, errors and all, without
  being able to read the schematics (Canticle for Leibowitz).
- *Carriage-watchers*: keep the braid timetable and hold markets on arrival days.

## Superconducting magic: the hard rule

Magitech is superconducting electronics on resonant crystal. The Anchor had two
technologies: **semiconductors** for bulk computing and power (what you rebuild), and
**superconductors** for wands, wards and lifts (what you inherit). Real physics, one
invented material:

> **Resonant crystal superconducts at ambient temperature while it is held at its
> resonant frequency.** Detune it, heat it or strain it, and it quenches.

Everything else follows from real superconductor physics:

| Real effect | In the game |
| --- | --- |
| Persistent current: a current in a closed superconducting loop flows without loss | **mana**: sparks stored in a crystal ring do not decay while it holds resonance |
| Single flux quantum: the smallest unit of magnetic flux a loop can hold | a **spark** is one flux quantum. The tithe was counted in quanta. A tally is a count of them |
| Josephson junction and RSFQ logic: superconducting digital circuits switching on single flux pulses | **wand cards**: spells are RSFQ routines. Modifiers are gates. A wand is a superconducting processor |
| Quench: a superconductor going normal releases its stored energy as heat | **Unstable**, **Overcharge**, a wand breaking: a quench. Big crystal ring quenches are explosions |
| Meissner effect: magnetic fields are expelled | **Mirror ward**: a field-expulsion shell that rejects incoming bolts |
| Flux pinning: a superconductor locks in place above a magnet | **Leap**, floating stones, lift cradles that hang still; the chain lifts used it to hold loads |
| Critical current and critical field | every wand has a limit. Overcharge pushes past it, and the card pays |

Consequences to keep consistent:

- Fire is the natural enemy of a wand: heat detunes crystal. Rime districts are where the
  Anchor kept crystal, and why the rime geode vaults exist.
- Magic is quantised. Costs, tallies and the tithe are integer counts of sparks, which is
  why the Anchor could keep exact ledgers of something as slippery as energy.
- The Bloom eats semiconductor metal layers, not resonant crystal. That is why wands
  survived and computers did not, and why the valley has magic before it has chips.
- What the Anchor never solved, and the player might: making resonant crystal from
  scratch. Today every crystal is mined or salvaged. Open slot.

## Real chipmaking: the process vocabulary

The tiers table above sets the order. These are the real steps, so machines, items and
Records can use correct names. Each step is a candidate machine or machine mode.

| Step | Real process | Notes for the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purify | quartz to metallurgical silicon (arc furnace), then the Siemens process to polysilicon | purity is counted in nines: 9N is 99.9999999% |
| Grow | Czochralski pulling of a single crystal boule; float-zone for the purest | a seed crystal is dipped and slowly drawn; a tense, slow machine |
| Wafer | saw, lap, polish | the first item that is obviously "high tech" |
| Oxidise | thermal oxide in a furnace | silicon's own glass; the reason silicon won |
| Pattern | coat photoresist, expose through a mask, develop | the light used sets the smallest feature (see tiers) |
| Etch | wet acid, later plasma (reactive ion) etching | removes what the pattern exposed |
| Dope | diffusion furnaces, later ion implantation | turns silicon into transistors; boron and phosphorus |
| Deposit | CVD, PVD (sputtering), later ALD one atom layer at a time | builds up films |
| Connect | aluminium wiring, then copper damascene with CMP polish | the metal layers are exactly what the Bloom eats |
| Test | wafer probe, then yield maps | a yield of 30% on a new node is normal and miserable |
| Package | dice, bond wires, encapsulate | a chip becomes a part you can hold |
| Clean | cleanroom classes (particles per volume), gowning, air showers | in this world, also quarantine against the Bloom |

Superconducting counterpart (Anchor wand cards): niobium films, aluminium oxide
barriers a few atoms thick, niobium again: the real Josephson junction stack. The
Anchor did this on resonant crystal instead of silicon. A late game "card fab" is the
same process line with a different substrate.

Writers: name the real step. "Spin-coated resist, exposed at 248 nm" is more alien and
more interesting than any invented term.

## After the fall: the long quench

Between the Ballast and the valley lie centuries nobody recorded well. People call them
**the long quench**, after what happens to a crystal ring when its resonance fails: the
stored energy leaves all at once as heat, and then there is nothing.

The world is not empty. It is full of people who live well enough without science and
have reasons to keep it that way. Magic survived the fall, because the Bloom ate
semiconductor metal and left resonant crystal alone. Automation did not. So for
centuries power has belonged to whoever holds working crystal.

### A rough history, in Records

Dates are counted in **descents**: arrivals of the braid carriage, one every three days.
Every literate culture since the fall has counted them, because nothing else in the sky
was reliable. Big numbers are approximate on purpose; different Records disagree.

| Era | Roughly | What happened | What it left |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Anchor | before the count | chips, cards, the tithe, the braid at full service | offices, the Undercrown, fabs |
| The Bloom | descent 0 is the first Record of grey boards | the factories die in a single generation; the keepers go up | quarantine mazes, taped doors, the last ascent |
| The Ballast | about 0 to 20 000 | the stayers keep the anchor weighted and the ledger written | kitchens in machines, stills, catacombs |
| The quench | about 20 000 to 90 000 | crystal rings fail one by one; knowledge of how to make them is lost; the first wand-lords rise | hill forts on office ruins, the first orders |
| The holds | about 90 000 to now | medieval and magical polities settle into a stable, unequal world | castles, monasteries, roads, markets on arrival days |
| Now | | you arrive with a workshop, and the factory starts | the valley you build |

Leave room in every row. A writer can add a war, a plague, a second Bloom, a failed
attempt to rebuild a fab, or a short-lived empire, so long as it leaves an object behind.

## The living powers

Factions are slots with a temperament, a source of power, and a reason to love or fear
what the player is building. Names below are working names; replace freely.

| Power | Kind | Holds power through | Attitude to automation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wand-lords** | medieval nobility | inherited working cards and crystal rings; a lord is someone whose wand still charges | threatened. Every card you can make devalues theirs |
| **The Orders of Resonance** | magical clergy | they can keep a ring at resonance without understanding why; ritual is procedure they no longer read | split. Some see your fab as heresy, some as the return of the rite |
| **The Copyists** | monastic scholars | they copy Anchor ledgers and schematics by hand, errors included | hungry. They have the drawings and no way to use them |
| **Carriage-watchers** | market towns | they keep the braid timetable and run the arrival-day fairs | opportunistic. Trade grows when you can build receivers |
| **The Tally-free** | hill clans, outlaws | they refuse the old tithe and anything that counts them | hostile to ledgers, friendly to anyone who leaves them alone |
| **Bloom-keepers** | fungal cults, deep villages | they live with the Bloom, eat it, and guard where it grows | fear the cleanroom. A fab is a wall against what they worship |
| **Salvage guilds** | city crafts | they dig ancient circuits and sell them at monopoly prices | threatened, then useful. They become your first suppliers or your first saboteurs |

Write each faction as people with a working economy, not villains. They kept the world
going through the quench. RimWorld's rule applies: a faction's opinion of you is a
number, and you earn it by what you trade and what you build near them.

### Holds and towns

Medieval settlements sit on top of Anchor ruins, because the ruins have water, stone and
sometimes a working ring. Settlement grammar:

- **A hold** is a fort on an office. The keep is the old ledger room. The lord's wand
  hangs in the place where the office's master crystal was.
- **A monastery** of an Order is a resonance hall: a crystal cathedral with a roof
  added. Services are maintenance done by ear.
- **A market town** sits under a braid corridor. Its calendar is the timetable.
- **A deep village** lives inside the Undercrown, as the Ballast did, and does not like
  visitors from the surface.

Open slots: a capital; a holy city; a war between holds over one working ring; a town that
was built on a Surveyor floor and slowly realised the floor is not natural.

## What is still in the sky

The Anchor and the Lifted left infrastructure that still works, half works, or falls.
All of it is visible at night if you know where to look.

| Leftover | State | What people below think it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The braid and its carriages | working, on schedule | weather, a god, a calendar |
| Talon and Harbour crowns | lit, silent | the houses of the Lifted, or the dead |
| The Loom orbital foundry | running something; nobody knows what | a star that moves wrong |
| The Continuum twin drums | counter-rotating, occasionally glinting | the twins, in several religions |
| Dead satellites and debris | decaying; some re-enter | **meteors**. The deorbit card calls them down on purpose |
| Mirror arrays | a few still track the sun | the false dawn some nights before the real one |
| Broken braid stubs | other corridors that snapped long ago; ruin lines across the land | giants' ropes, roads to nowhere |

Rules:

- Nothing up there talks to people below. Communication with the braid is a thing the
  player rebuilds through the radio tiers, and it answers in contracts and manifests.
- Every sky object should be reachable by the radio ladder eventually, and some should
  stay unreachable on purpose.

## Why you

The player is the first person in a long time to build a machine that makes another
machine. Who they are is a Rumour, never a Fact. Three Rumours to plant and never
settle:

- **The commissioned technician.** The braid sends a commissioning grant (ML-C01)
  because something above has decided the anchor is staffed again. Maybe it was waiting
  for someone to light the workshop heart.
- **The heir.** The scraped name in the Ballast ledger was yours, or your line's.
- **Nobody special.** You found a workshop, and you were stubborn. The Ballast would have
  liked that best.

Whatever is true, the world treats you by what you build.

## The return of automation, as a story

Each tech tier changes the world, not only the player's base. Use the tiers table as the
spine and let the factions react. These are beats to build toward, not a script.

| Tier | What the player gains | What the world notices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Salvage | a workshop heart, ancient circuits | salvage guilds raise their prices |
| 1 Crystal detector | a radio; the braid timetable heard directly | carriage-watchers come to trade; Orders take an interest |
| 2 Transistor | refined silicon, controlled furnaces | Copyists bring drawings they cannot use |
| 3 Planar IC | the first made circuit | wand-lords send envoys, then threats; the first Bloom outbreak near your clean air |
| 4–5 VLSI and copper | automation at scale | holds begin to depend on your trade; one tries to take your factory instead |
| 6 Immersion | world-class water and optics | the sluice works can be restored; deep villages notice their water changing |
| 7 EUV | the hardest machine on the planet | the Loom changes its orbit. Something above is paying attention |
| 8 Orbit | wafers ride the braid down | you can go up, and the Lifted have to decide what to say |

Also open: a card fab (superconducting cards made from scratch) would break the
wand-lords for good. Whether the player builds one, sells cards, or keeps the secret is
the biggest political choice the world can offer. Leave it to the player.

## The ninth office

The game's central secret, and the first one a player should notice.

Eight offices are known: Artificers, Watch, Sluice, Founders, Keepers, Archivists, Toll,
Choir. Records keep pointing at a **ninth**. An office directory has a ninth line painted
over. An index card says "Ninth office: see Return", and the card for Return is missing.
A Ballast door note says "Gone to the ninth." An artificer's work order says "Coil for the
ninth office. Do not log."

Rules:

- **Fact:** a ninth office exists, called Return, and it was not on any map on purpose.
- What it did is an open slot. Write toward it, never into it. Plausible readings that
  Records can support, and contradict: it ran the braid's return leg (the momentum
  ledger); it was where the Ballast went; it was the Bloom's origin; it was the Lifted's
  way back down.
- Never put it on the surface. If it is ever found, it is deep, and a whole update.
- At most one Record in ten mentions it. The hook works because it is rare.

Why it works for science fiction and fantasy readers: it is the missing room in the
house plan. Familiar shape (the secret level, the thirteenth floor), but the clues are
bureaucratic, not mystical. A painted-over directory line is more unsettling than a
prophecy.

## Mystery design: how to be cryptic without trying too hard

Noita, Outer Wilds and Hyper Light Drifter hide their best material in plain sight.
Rules we follow:

- **Clues are objects with a use.** A gauge card, a toll board, a tuning chart. The
  mystery is in the margin, not the headline.
- **One wrong detail per text.** A carriage lamp that "showed two colours". A name on
  both lists. A number crossed out and raised. The player notices the detail; the text
  never points at it.
- **Repeat, do not explain.** The same mark at the same spacing; the same coupling number
  in two sites. Recognition is the reward.
- **Let numbers carry it.** Descent 18 211. Strand 31. Ring 4. Coupling 7 of 12. Numbers
  invite players to cross-reference, and to build wikis.
- **No answer in one place.** Each thread needs at least three Records in three
  different strata before it resolves into a guess.
- **Familiar, then turned.** Use a shape fans know (the missing office, the forbidden
  strain, the ship that keeps arriving) and turn it with one concrete, physical
  detail (it is a filing error; it eats copper; it is on a timetable).

## Records in the world (implemented)

`src/world/lore.rs` places Records in containers. When the player opens a salvage
crate, survey cache or cultivator cache, about half of them add one line after the loot
text. The line is chosen from:

- the place name from `sites::location`, which picks the writer (an office, the Ballast,
  the Anchor, the Surveyors, or the Holds on the surface);
- depth, when the place name does not decide it (deeper is older);
- the container's cell and the world seed, so the same coffer always holds the same
  paper.

It reads no save data and changes no terrain sampling. To add Records, append to a pool
in `lore.rs`: keep each line under 100 characters (a test checks it), in the
maintenance register, and give it one wrong detail. To add a writer, add a `Pool` and a
place-name rule.

## The sky runs on one clock (implemented)

Every orbital object (skyhooks, the Loom, the Continuum drums) uses the same orbital
clock: real seconds, with the planet turning once per sidereal day. The sun runs on the
fast game day. So orbits move at real speed (a low orbit crosses the sky in minutes) and
all orbital objects agree on where the ground is. Earlier, the megastructures ran on a
clock that counted 86 400 s per game day, so they raced across the sky while the hooks
crawled. Use `megastructures::seconds` or `skyhook::clock` for anything new in orbit.
